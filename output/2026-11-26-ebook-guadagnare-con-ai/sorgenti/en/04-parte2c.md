# Creating Images and Graphics with AI {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

Image generation tools create illustrations, realistic photographs, backgrounds, textures, icons and concepts from a text description or a reference image. AI-powered editors also let you **remove backgrounds**, **extend** an image, **replace** an object, upscale resolution or correct lighting.

There are two ways to earn:

1. **Design services** for businesses and professionals: social media graphics, banners, website images, product mockups, presentations, event materials.
2. **Visual products**: prints, illustrations for digital products, packs of backgrounds or design elements (pay attention to platform rules and rights, see below).

## Copyright on AI images: what to know first

- In the United States, the Copyright Office reaffirmed in January 2025 that images generated entirely by AI from a simple prompt **cannot be protected** by copyright; human contributions (selection, arrangement, significant creative changes) can be, assessed case by case.
- In the European Union and in Italy, copyright requires a work resulting from human creativity; the status of works generated with AI is the subject of debate and legislation. In Italy, the artificial intelligence law approved in 2025 reaffirmed the requirement of human creative input.
- **Practical consequence:** you can't easily stop others from reusing an image generated entirely by AI. The value of your work lies in the **overall design**, consistency with the brand and the creative changes.
- Each generator's **terms of use** state whether and how you can use images commercially. Some tools require specific plans for companies above certain revenue thresholds (Midjourney, for example, requires the Pro or Mega plan for companies with revenue above $1 million).

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** Don't generate images that imitate brands, logos, protected characters or the recognizable style of living artists for commercial purposes, and don't create realistic images of real people without their consent. These are the most common causes of legal problems and removal from platforms.
:::

## Why someone would pay

A business doesn't pay for "an image": it pays for **images consistent with its brand**, delivered in the right formats, on time, without errors (deformed hands, misspelled text, altered products) and with the assurance that it can use them legally. Someone who knows how to check and refine AI output offers exactly that assurance.

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Learn design basics**: hierarchy (what the eye sees first), contrast, alignment, white space, a maximum of two fonts.
2. **Choose one generation tool** and one **layout tool** (for example Midjourney + Canva, or ChatGPT + Canva).
3. **Create a "style recipe"**: a base prompt with style, lighting, palette and framing that ensures consistency across several images.
4. **Run a quality check** on every image: hands, eyes, text, proportions, duplicated objects, background details, consistency with the real product.
5. **Build a portfolio** of 3 complete projects (not scattered images): for example "social identity for a café," "visuals for a product launch," "illustrations for a children's book."
6. **Set up a delivery process**: formats (square, 4:5, 9:16, 16:9), resolutions, file names, a shared folder.

## How to find your first clients

- Small businesses using low-quality photos or inconsistent graphics.
- Professionals launching a service or an event.
- Independent authors looking for illustrations or covers (with AI-use disclosure where required, as on KDP).
- Freelance platforms (Fiverr, Upwork, Malt): high competition, useful for first jobs and first reviews.

The ranges are indicative. Always include a defined number of **revisions** in the price (for example 2) and specify the **usage rights** granted.

## How to make your first sale

Offer a limited **trial package**: "5 coordinated graphics for your next posts, delivered in 5 days, 2 revisions included." Show an example made with their logo and their real products (using photos taken from their profile, only as a draft for the pitch).

## Complete practical example

**Davide** (a fictional character) is a photography enthusiast who works at a travel agency.

1. **Niche:** accommodation businesses (B&Bs, farm stays, holiday homes).
2. **Service:** "Seasonal visual kit": 12 social graphics and 3 website banners, starting from the property's **real photos** (improved with AI for lighting and cleanup, without altering rooms, views or amenities) and generated decorative backgrounds.
3. **Ethical rule:** never show amenities, views or furnishings the property doesn't have. A misleading photo in a travel listing is misleading advertising.
4. **Price:** €350 per season.

### What we learned
- The value isn't the single image but consistency, quality control and safe usage.
- Images generated entirely by AI have limited legal protection: human creative contribution is what counts.
- Tools' terms of use govern commercial use.
- Images of real products must not be altered.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Delivering images with obvious defects.
- Imitating brands, protected characters or recognizable artists.
- Creating realistic images of real people without consent.
- Misleadingly altering real products, places or services.
- Not specifying revisions and usage rights.

### Practical exercise

# Producing Promotional Videos and Marketing Content {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

This service consists of producing **short videos** to promote products, services, events or brands: videos for Instagram and TikTok, ads, videos for websites and presentations, product videos, tutorials, (real) testimonials edited professionally.

AI is involved at many stages: ideas and scripts, voiceover generation, automatic captions, assisted editing, generated or library music, generation of short clips or animations, extending or cleaning up footage, digital avatars that read a script.

## Why someone would pay

Video is the most used format on social media, but producing it well takes time and skills small businesses don't have. They pay for:

- a **clear message** in the first few seconds;
- **professional** visual and audio quality;
- the **right formats** for each platform;
- **fast**, regular delivery.

## Tools

| Category | Tools | Indicative prices (November 2026, public sources) |
|---|---|---|
| Editing | CapCut, DaVinci Resolve (free), Descript, Adobe Premiere | CapCut has a free plan and Pro (prices vary by country); Descript from about $16–24/month |
| Video generation | Runway, Kling, Google Veo (via Gemini or Flow), Sora (OpenAI) | Runway Standard about $15/month; Kling Standard about $7/month; often credit-based |
| Voiceover | ElevenLabs | Starter about $6/month; Creator $22/month |
| Talking avatars | HeyGen, Synthesia | Limited free plans and paid plans |
| Music | Licensed libraries (included in some apps), AI music generators | Check the commercial license |
| Captions | Automatic in CapCut and social apps | Included |

Video tool prices change very often and sources don't always agree: always check the official page and the **credit cost** of each generation.

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to get started | 3–6 weeks to master editing and workflow |
| Skills | Editing rhythm, writing short scripts, clean audio, visual sense |
| Investment | €0–80/month (tools) + optional microphone and light (€50–150 one-off) |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Learn the structure of an effective short video**:
   - 0–3 seconds: visual and verbal hook;
   - 3–15 seconds: problem or desire;
   - 15–40 seconds: solution shown (not just described);
   - close: a clear call to action.
2. **Learn one editing tool** thoroughly: cuts, rhythm, animated text, captions, music, 9:16 export.
3. **Use AI for the script** and for the **voiceover** when the client's voice isn't available.
4. **Combine real footage** of the product or service with generated clips for backgrounds, transitions or concept scenes.
5. **Create an approval process**: brief → script → draft → revision → final.
6. **Build a portfolio** of 3–5 videos for different sectors or the same sector.

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** Realistic AI-generated people or digital avatars in an ad must be recognizable as artificial content when they could mislead: in the EU, deepfakes must be disclosed (AI Act, Art. 50) and platforms require labels. Never use a real person's voice or face without written consent. And **never** show a product different from the real one: if a garment, a dish or a place is altered, the ad becomes misleading.
:::

## How to find your first clients

- Businesses that already publish videos, but of low quality: offer to remake one of their videos as an example.
- Communications agencies and social media managers who need an external editor.
- Local events, gyms, restaurants, real estate agencies (property presentation videos with real footage).
- Freelance platforms for first jobs.

## How to set prices

| Service | Indicative range |
|---|---|
| Editing a short video from the client's footage | €40–120 |
| Complete short promo video (script, voice, editing, captions) | €150–500 |
| Monthly package (4–8 short videos) | €400–1,200/month |
| Company presentation video (60–120 seconds) | €500–2,000 depending on filming and complexity |

Always specify: number of revisions, formats delivered, music licenses, who provides the footage, timelines.

## How to make your first sale

Choose a business, download (with permission or from their public profile, only as a private draft) their existing photos and videos, and edit a 20-second video that's better than what they publish. Show it in person or privately: "I made this example for you. If you like it, I can make 4 a month."

# Offering Copywriting and Writing Services {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

**Copywriting** is writing with a commercial goal: getting people to buy, sign up, get in touch, book. It includes copy for websites, sales pages, emails, ads, product descriptions, social posts, brochures, video scripts. Alongside copywriting there are other writing services: blog articles (with attention to SEO, i.e. search engine optimization), newsletters, corporate copy, revision and editing, translation and localization.

AI has made producing drafts extremely fast. As a result the market has split: generic low-cost copy has largely been replaced by AI, while there's demand for professionals who can **understand the client, find the right message, verify and guarantee quality**.

## Why someone would pay

A business could use AI by itself. It pays a copywriter because:

- it doesn't know **what to say** (positioning, differences from competitors, customer objections);
- it doesn't have time to **guide and correct** the AI;
- it wants copy that's **consistent with its voice** and **accurate** in content;
- it wants someone accountable for the **result** (conversions, sign-ups).

## Tools

| Stage | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Research and drafts | Free AI assistants | AI assistants, Pro plans with projects per client |
| Keyword research | Google (suggestions, "People also ask"), Google Trends | Professional SEO tools |
| Proofreading | Google Docs and Word spell check, LanguageTool (free) | LanguageTool Premium, DeepL Write Pro |
| Translation | DeepL (free with limits) | DeepL Pro |
| Organization | Google Drive, Notion | Paid versions |

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to get started | 2–4 weeks for a portfolio and an offer |
| Skills | Clear writing, listening to the client, knowledge of the principles of persuasion, linguistic accuracy |
| Investment | €0–30/month |

## Copywriting principles to know

1. **Talk about the customer, not the company.** "Sleep without back pain" is stronger than "Our mattress has 7 zones."
2. **One text, one goal.** Every page or email must ask for one specific action.
3. **Specificity.** "48-hour delivery in Milan" is more credible than "fast delivery."
4. **The AIDA structure:** Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Or **PAS:** Problem, Agitation (why the problem matters), Solution.
5. **Proof.** Real reviews, verifiable numbers, guarantees, concrete examples.
6. **Objections.** Answer doubts in advance (price, timing, trust).

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Choose a specialization** by type of copy (sales pages, emails, e-commerce product pages) or by sector (tourism, health, technology, fashion).
2. **Create your AI workflow**:
   - a brief questionnaire for the client;
   - research: reviews of the client's customers, competitors, frequently asked questions;
   - using AI to extract benefits, objections and the customers' language;
   - 3 different messaging angles;
   - a draft with AI, **your own rewrite**, fact-checking, proofreading.
3. **Create a portfolio** of 3–5 pieces: as an exercise you can rewrite pages of existing businesses (without publishing them in their name), showing before and after.
4. **Prepare a price list** and a quote template.

## How to find your first clients

- Web and marketing agencies looking for collaborators.
- E-commerce stores with thin product pages or pages copied from the manufacturer.
- Professionals with outdated websites.
- LinkedIn: publish short "before/after" analyses of copy (without ridiculing anyone).
- Freelance platforms.

## How to set prices

| Service | Indicative range |
|---|---|
| E-commerce product page (150–300 words) | €15–40 |
| Optimized blog article (1,000–1,500 words) | €60–200 |
| Copy for a brochure website (5 pages) | €400–1,200 |
| Landing page / sales page | €300–1,500 |
| Sequence of 5 emails | €250–800 |

Indicative market ranges. Prefer **project pricing** to per-word pricing: with AI, words are cheap; the value lies in the strategy.

## How to make your first sale

Offer a **free 15-minute audit** of one page of a potential client's website, with 3 concrete improvements and a rewritten headline. If the client likes it, propose a full rewrite of the page at a fixed price.

## Complete practical example

**Andrea** (a fictional character) is a former real estate agent who writes well.

1. **Specialization:** copy for real estate agencies and property listings.
2. **Offer:** the "Listings people actually read" package: 10 listings a month, a rewrite of the "about us" page, 4 emails for prospective buyers.
3. **Process:** the agency sends the technical sheet, photos and notes; Andrea uses AI for a structured draft, then checks every detail (square meters, energy rating, distances) against the documentation and rewrites it in a consistent tone.
4. **Rule:** no information that isn't in the documentation; no discriminatory or misleading wording.
5. **Price:** €450/month.

### What we learned
- Generic copywriting has been replaced by AI; strategic, verified copywriting is in demand.
- The value lies in understanding the client, finding the message and guaranteeing accuracy.
- AIDA and PAS are simple, effective structures.
- Project pricing reflects value better than per-word pricing.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Delivering generated copy without rewriting and checking it.
- Including unverified data, reviews or promises.
- Competing on price with people who sell generic copy.
- Writing for everyone instead of a specific audience.

### Practical exercise
