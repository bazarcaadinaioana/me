# Mistakes, Risks and the Future {.part}

A business that lasts is built on the rules. This part goes deeper into legal, financial and reputational risks, the tax questions to clarify in your own country and how to stay up to date without depending on tools. The information is general and current as of November 2026: it doesn't replace advice from a professional.

# Scams, Rights, Privacy and Transparency {.chapter}

## Online scams and false income promises

The "make money with AI" topic also attracts scammers. Recognizing them saves you money and time.

### Warning signs

| Sign | Why it's suspicious |
|---|---|
| Promises of precise, fast income ("€10,000 a month in 60 days") | Nobody can guarantee results: they depend on too many factors |
| Income screenshots as the only proof | Easy to manipulate; they don't show costs, refunds, taxes |
| Artificial urgency ("today only," "last 3 places" repeated) | Designed to stop you thinking |
| The "secret method" isn't explained before purchase | It often doesn't exist |
| The main income comes from recruiting other people | A typical structure of pyramid schemes |
| Requests for payment in cryptocurrency or to anonymous foreign accounts | Makes recovery impossible |
| Software or "bots" that promise to generate income by themselves | Often scams, sometimes malware |
| "AI trading" investments with guaranteed returns | In finance, guaranteed returns are a classic sign of fraud; always check authorization with the regulators (in Italy, Consob publishes a list of blocked websites and of authorized intermediaries) |

### Other common scams

- **Clone sites** of famous AI tools, reached through sponsored ads: they steal data and payments.
- **Fake job offers** as an "AI trainer" or "content evaluator" that ask for an upfront payment for training or equipment.
- **Fake customers** who send a payment larger than what's owed and ask for the difference back.
- **Phishing** of platform accounts (Etsy, Amazon, Meta) with emails imitating official communications.

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** Golden rule: legitimate platforms never ask for your password by email or message. Always sign in by typing the official address, turn on two-factor authentication on every account and use a password manager.
:::

### How you should behave

All the rules that apply to others apply to you too: **don't promise income** to your clients or students, **don't show unverifiable results**, **don't create false urgency**. In Italy and the EU unfair commercial practices are penalized (in Italy the Competition and Market Authority, AGCM, oversees them).

## Copyright and intellectual property

### What you can protect

- **Text, images and designs with human creative input** are generally protectable by copyright.
- **Content generated entirely by AI** from a simple prompt has uncertain or no protection in many jurisdictions. In the United States, the Copyright Office (report of January 2025) reaffirmed that human input is required; human contributions (selection, arrangement, creative modifications) can be protected case by case. In Italy, the 2025 artificial intelligence law confirmed that works are protected if they result from human intellectual effort, even when created with the help of AI.
- **Practical consequence:** document your creative process (drafts, notes, edits) and always add a significant personal contribution to the products you sell.

### What you can't use

- Other people's **registered trademarks** and trade names in your products (for example film characters, sports logos, famous brand names).
- Other people's **protected works** as a basis to copy or "rework" with AI.
- **The recognizable style of living artists** for commercial products that could be confused with their work: besides the legal risks, it's ethically wrong.
- **Images, fonts and music** without a commercial license.

### Tools' terms of use

Every tool's terms of use state who holds the rights to the output, whether you can use it commercially and within what limits. Read them before selling anything produced with that tool, and reread them when you change plans.

### Platform rules

| Platform | Key rule on AI (November 2026) |
|---|---|
| Amazon KDP | Mandatory disclosure of AI-generated content (text, images, translations); not required for content that is only AI-assisted |
| Etsy | Disclosure of AI use; ban on AI-generated functional guides (recipes, patterns, instructions) since 22 October 2026; ban on bot-created listings |
| Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube | Labels for realistic content generated or altered with AI; mandatory disclosure in certain cases |

## Privacy and data protection

In the European Union, the processing of personal data is governed by the **GDPR**. Some practical rules for anyone working with AI:

1. **Don't enter third parties' personal data** (clients, guests, patients, employees) into AI tools on consumer plans. If it's necessary, use business plans with contractual safeguards, and check where the data is processed and whether it's used for training.
2. **Always anonymize**: remove names, contact details, addresses and identifying details before analyzing texts with AI.
3. **Special categories of data** (health, sexual orientation, political and religious opinions, biometric data) require even greater caution: avoid processing them with AI tools unless you have specific advice.
4. If you process data on a client's behalf you're a **data processor** and a written agreement is required.
5. Your website and email list require a **privacy notice**, **consent** management and, if you use non-essential cookies, a compliant **cookie banner**.
6. People's **voice and image** are personal data: cloning a voice or creating an avatar of a real person requires their explicit consent.

In Italy the reference authority is the **Garante per la protezione dei dati personali** (garanteprivacy.it), which publishes practical guides.

## AI-generated content: transparency

The **EU Artificial Intelligence Regulation (AI Act)** sets out transparency obligations in **Article 50**, applicable from **2 August 2026** (with a transition period until 2 December 2026 for some obligations of providers of generative systems already on the market). In short, for people who use AI tools in their business:

- **deepfakes** (images, audio or video that realistically depict people, places or events and could appear authentic) must be disclosed as artificially generated or manipulated, with exceptions and lighter requirements for clearly artistic, satirical or creative works;
- **AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest** must be disclosed, unless it has undergone human review with editorial responsibility;
- systems that **interact with people** (such as chatbots) must make it clear that people are talking to an AI, when this isn't obvious.

The AI Act also requires (since 2 February 2025) that those who use AI systems in a professional context take measures to ensure a sufficient level of **AI literacy** among their staff.

::: {.box .tip}
**Tip.** Even when the law doesn't require it, transparency pays off: a short note such as "Images created with the help of artificial intelligence" or "Text written with the support of AI tools and reviewed by [name]" protects your customers' trust. For the regulatory details, see the official text of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and the European Commission's guidelines.
:::

## Quality and verification of information

The most frequent risk isn't legal but one of **quality**: publishing or selling wrong information. Always apply this protocol:

1. **Identify the verifiable facts** in the text (numbers, dates, names, regulations, prices, quotes, technical, health, legal or tax advice).
2. **Check them against primary sources**: official websites, legal texts, tool documentation, publications from recognized bodies.
3. **Note the source and the date** of the check.
4. **Flag uncertainty** when sources disagree.
5. **Have specialist content reviewed** (health, law, tax, finance, safety) by a professional in the field.
6. **Update** products when information changes.

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- Scams on the "AI and income" theme have recognizable signs.
- Copyright protection requires human creative input; other people's trademarks and works are off-limits.
- The GDPR requires caution with personal data in AI tools.
- The AI Act requires transparency on deepfakes, chatbots and some public-interest texts from 2 August 2026.
- Verifying information is a procedure, not an option.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Believing promises of guaranteed income.
- Selling products featuring protected brands or characters.
- Entering clients' personal data into consumer tools.
- Publishing realistic generated content without disclosing it.

### Practical exercise
Take a piece of content you created with AI (a text or an image). Apply the 6-step verification protocol and the rights check: tool licenses, trademarks, people depicted, required disclosures. Note what you had to correct.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've turned on two-factor authentication on every account.
- [ ] I've read the commercial terms of use of the tools I use.
- [ ] I don't enter third parties' personal data into consumer tools.
- [ ] I know when I must disclose AI use.
- [ ] I apply an information verification protocol.
:::

# Taxes, Independence from Tools and the Future {.chapter}

## Tax and regulatory questions to look into

Any income, even small, can have tax and social security consequences. The rules change from country to country and over time: this section gives you **the questions to ask** a professional, using Italy as an example.

### Questions to ask your accountant

1. Is my business **occasional** or **habitual**? (The deciding factor is continuity and organization, not just the amount.)
2. Do I need to register for **VAT** or as self-employed? With which **activity code**?
3. Which **tax regime** is suitable (for example the flat-rate regime in Italy)?
4. Which **social security contributions** must I pay, and to which scheme?
5. How is **VAT** handled on sales to consumers in other EU countries (the OSS scheme) and outside the EU?
6. How do I declare income from **foreign platforms** (Etsy, Amazon, Gumroad, Payhip)?
7. Do the platforms **report my data** to the tax authorities? (In the EU, the DAC7 directive requires digital platforms to report the data of certain sellers.)
8. Which **costs** can I deduct (subscriptions, equipment)?

### The example of Italy (general information, November 2026)

- **Occasional self-employment (prestazione occasionale):** the oft-quoted €5,000 annual limit concerns the obligation to register with INPS's Gestione Separata (the social security scheme) for occasional self-employed workers; it is **not** a threshold that exempts you from registering for VAT. An organized, continuous activity (for example an online store with a stable catalog and repeat sales) can be considered habitual even below that figure.
- **Flat-rate regime (regime forfettario):** revenue limit of €85,000 a year; substitute tax of 15%, reduced to 5% for the first five years of new businesses that meet the requirements; taxable income is calculated by applying to revenue a **profitability coefficient** that depends on the ATECO activity code. Social security contributions are paid separately.
- **E-commerce:** selling digital products is a commercial activity with specific VAT rules.

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** This information is simplified and only meant to orient you. Thresholds, rates and interpretations change: consult an accountant, a tax assistance center or your trade association **before** you start selling. If you live outside Italy, look for the equivalent information from your country's tax authority.
:::

### Other regulatory aspects

- **Consumer rights:** pre-contractual information, the right of withdrawal (with the exceptions for digital content), guarantees.
- **Advertising:** the obligation to make advertising recognizable (for example in collaborations with brands on social media).
- **Professional liability insurance:** consider it for consulting, automation and managing company accounts.

## How to avoid depending entirely on AI tools

Tools change their prices, rules and quality. Platforms change their algorithms. A robust business reduces this dependence.

1. **Transferable skills.** Invest in what stays valid with any tool: understanding customers, writing well, designing, selling, verifying.
2. **More than one tool for every critical task.** Know at least one alternative for writing, design and video.
3. **Your own assets.** Keep your prompts, templates, style guides and product source files in your own archives (with backups).
4. **Your own audience.** Your email list and direct customer contacts don't depend on an algorithm.
5. **Several sales channels**, once the first one works.
6. **Documented processes.** If a tool disappears, you must be able to rebuild your work.
7. **Human value.** Relationships, responsibility, taste and experience can't be replaced by a subscription.

## How to stay up to date

The opposite risk to dependence is the anxiety of chasing every new release. A simple system:

| Frequency | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Read 1–2 reliable newsletters on AI and your market | 30 minutes |
| Monthly | Check news, prices and terms of the tools you use | 1 hour |
| Monthly | Try **one** new feature on a real task | 1 hour |
| Quarterly | Review subscriptions, platform rules and regulatory obligations | 2 hours |
| Yearly | Update the products you sell with new information | Varies |

**Recommended (primary) sources:** official blogs and release notes from AI providers; platform help centers (Etsy, KDP, Meta, TikTok, YouTube); the European Commission's website for the AI Act; the Garante privacy; the Agenzia delle Entrate and INPS (or their equivalents in your country).

## The future: what to expect

Without making predictions that can't be verified, some trends are already visible at the end of 2026:

- **AI agents** increasingly able to carry out multi-step tasks (searching, filling in, organizing) under human supervision.
- **Integration** of AI into all everyday work tools.
- **More rules and more transparency**: content labeling, platform obligations, attention to copyright.
- **Saturation of generic content** and greater value for what is authentic, specific, verified and signed by real people.

The guiding principle of this book remains valid in this scenario too: **use AI to multiply your value, not to replace it**.

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- Before selling, you need clear answers on occasional vs. habitual activity, VAT registration, tax regime, contributions and VAT.
- In Italy, the €5,000 limit concerns occasional self-employed workers' INPS contributions, not an exemption from VAT registration.
- Transferable skills, your own assets and your own audience reduce dependence on tools.
- A regular update routine avoids both obsolescence and new-release anxiety.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Starting to sell without clarifying your tax position.
- Believing there are no obligations below a certain amount.
- Depending on a single tool or a single channel.
- Chasing every new release without a method.

# Conclusion {.unnumbered .front}

You've reached the end of this book, but the beginning of the real work. You've seen that artificial intelligence isn't a money-making machine, but an extraordinary tool in the hands of people who know what they want to achieve, for whom, and to what standard of quality.

Let's sum up what you've learned in seven principles:

1. **Start from a real problem** of real people, not from the tool.
2. **Specialize**: a clear niche makes everything easier.
3. **Validate before you build**: interviews, prototypes, pilot editions.
4. **Use AI to accelerate, not to replace** experience, judgment and responsibility.
5. **Check everything** you publish or sell.
6. **Respect the rules**: platforms, rights, privacy, transparency, taxes.
7. **Build assets of your own**: skills, processes, audience, products.

The 30-day plan is waiting for you. Start tomorrow, with day 1. One hour a day, consistently, produces results no shortcut can give you.
