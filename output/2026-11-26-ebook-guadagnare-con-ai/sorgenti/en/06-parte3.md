# The AI Tools You Need to Know {.part}

This part is a reference guide. You don't need to use every tool: for each category, one or two are enough. For each tool you'll find what it's for, how to use it, a practical example, pros and cons, prices and free alternatives.

::: {.box .warn}
**How to read the prices.** Prices were checked in November 2026 on official pages or, where indicated, on third-party public sources. They're in US dollars when the provider publishes prices in dollars; in Europe the final price may differ because of exchange rates and VAT. Many tools change their plans several times a year: **always check the official page before buying**. Where we write "check," sources disagreed or the figure couldn't be confirmed.
:::

# A Practical Guide to Tools by Category {.chapter}

## How to choose a tool

Before subscribing, answer five questions:

1. **What precise task must it perform?** (not "do design," but "create 12 coordinated posts a month")
2. **Is the free plan enough to test it seriously?**
3. **Can I use what it produces commercially?** (read the terms of use)
4. **Where does my data, and my clients' data, end up?**
5. **Can I cancel easily?**

## Writing and research

### ChatGPT

- **What it's for:** writing, reasoning, file analysis, web search, images, voice.
- **How to use it:** type a request in the chat; for recurring work, create a project with instructions and files.
- **Practical example:** you upload a client's price list and ask: "Create 10 post ideas for November, one for each service, with a title and 60 words of copy."
- **Pros:** versatility, built-in image generation, large ecosystem.
- **Cons:** recognizable default style; usage limits on the free plan.
- **Prices:** free; Go $8/month; Plus $20/month; Pro from $100/month. Business and Enterprise plans for companies.
- **Free alternative:** the free plan itself, or the other assistants.

### Claude

- **What it's for:** polished writing, analysis of long documents, working on projects with reference documents.
- **How to use it:** chat; projects with saved instructions and knowledge; uploading PDFs and documents.
- **Practical example:** you upload an 80-page internal manual and ask for a 2-page quick guide for new hires.
- **Pros:** writing quality, adherence to instructions, handling long texts.
- **Cons:** doesn't generate images; usage limits on the free plan.
- **Prices:** free; Pro $20/month (monthly); Max $100 or $200/month. Team and Enterprise plans for companies.
- **Free alternative:** the free plan.

### Gemini

- **What it's for:** an assistant integrated with Gmail, Docs, Drive, YouTube.
- **How to use it:** from the Gemini app or directly inside Google tools.
- **Practical example:** in Gmail you ask for a draft reply to a quote request using information from a document in Drive.
- **Pros:** Google integration; the subscription includes storage.
- **Cons:** features and prices vary by country.
- **Prices:** free; Google AI Plus (€4.99/month in some EU countries); Google AI Pro ($19.99/month in the US, price by country in Europe); Ultra.
- **Free alternative:** the free plan.

### Perplexity

- **What it's for:** research with cited sources.
- **How to use it:** ask a question; the answer includes links to the sources, which you should open and check.
- **Practical example:** "What are Etsy's rules on digital products created with AI? Cite only pages on etsy.com."
- **Pros:** visible sources, fast for market research.
- **Cons:** it can summarize sources badly: they need to be opened.
- **Prices:** free; Pro $20/month (third-party sources); Max for heavy use.
- **Free alternative:** the web search built into free assistants.

### NotebookLM

- **What it's for:** studying and querying only the documents you upload.
- **How to use it:** create a "notebook," upload PDFs, websites, videos; ask questions and generate summaries, maps, audio overviews.
- **Practical example:** you upload 10 reviews of competing books and ask for the most criticized themes.
- **Pros:** answers grounded in the documents, with references.
- **Cons:** only as useful as the documents you provide.
- **Prices:** free with limits; higher limits with Google AI subscriptions.
- **Free alternative:** it's already free.

## Image generation

### Midjourney

- **What it's for:** high-aesthetic-quality images.
- **How to use it:** on the website, writing descriptions and using reference images for style and subject.
- **Practical example:** a consistent series of 8 illustrations for a planner, with the same palette and style.
- **Pros:** aesthetic quality, consistency of style, large community.
- **Cons:** no stable free plan; images are public on the basic plans (private mode only on Pro and Mega).
- **Prices:** Basic $10/month, Standard $30, Pro $60, Mega $120; 20% off with annual billing.
- **Free alternative:** generators built into free assistants; Adobe Firefly's free plan.

### Generators built into assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini)

- **What it's for:** creating and editing images conversationally.
- **How to use it:** "Create an image of…," then "Change the background to…," "Make it vertical."
- **Practical example:** an illustration for a post and its vertical version for stories.
- **Pros:** simplicity, edits in words, improved text in images.
- **Cons:** limits on free plans; less fine control.
- **Prices:** included in the assistants' plans.
- **Free alternative:** the free plans themselves.

### Adobe Firefly

- **What it's for:** generation and editing built into Photoshop, Express and Illustrator.
- **How to use it:** generative fill (select an area and describe what to insert), text-to-image generation.
- **Practical example:** extending the background of a product photo to fit a 16:9 format.
- **Pros:** integration with professional tools; Adobe states it trains on licensed content.
- **Cons:** credit system; the full Adobe tools are expensive.
- **Prices:** free plan with limited credits; credit-based paid plans (check).
- **Free alternative:** Photopea + free generators.

### Ideogram

- **What it's for:** images with readable text (posters, invitations, logo concepts).
- **How to use it:** a description with the text in quotation marks.
- **Practical example:** a poster reading "Christmas Market – December 14" in an illustrated style.
- **Pros:** handles text in images.
- **Cons:** less versatile style for realistic photography.
- **Prices:** free plan; paid plans (check).
- **Free alternative:** the free plan.

## Video creation

### Runway

- **What it's for:** generating video clips from text or images, AI editing tools.
- **How to use it:** upload an image and describe the movement ("the camera slowly moves toward the cup").
- **Practical example:** a 5-second clip to open a promotional video.
- **Pros:** advanced creative tools.
- **Cons:** credits run out fast; several attempts are needed.
- **Prices:** free with one-time credits; Standard about $15/month, Pro about $35/month (third-party source, September 2026; check).
- **Free alternative:** initial free credits; simple animations in Canva or CapCut.

### Kling

- **What it's for:** generating realistic videos from text or images.
- **How to use it:** a descriptive prompt or a starting image; choice of duration and format.
- **Practical example:** a 5–10 second concept scene for an ad.
- **Pros:** realism, low entry price.
- **Cons:** credit system; variable generation times.
- **Prices:** Standard about $7/month (third-party sources, 2026; check).
- **Free alternative:** daily or initial free credits, if available.

### Google Veo and OpenAI Sora

- **What they're for:** high-quality video generation, including audio.
- **How to use them:** through the apps and subscriptions of their respective providers (Gemini/Flow for Veo, ChatGPT/the Sora app for Sora).
- **Pros:** high quality, integration with the assistants.
- **Cons:** usage limits tied to the plan; availability varies by country.
- **Prices:** included with limits in some subscriptions (check).
- **Free alternative:** editing real footage in CapCut.

### HeyGen and Synthesia (avatars)

- **What they're for:** videos with digital avatars reading a script, including in multiple languages.
- **Practical example:** an internal training video translated into three languages.
- **Pros:** speed for informational videos.
- **Cons:** artificial feel; transparency obligations; less suited to emotional content.
- **Prices:** limited free plans and paid plans (check).
- **Free alternative:** recording yourself with your phone.

## Editing

### CapCut

- **What it's for:** editing videos for social media, automatic captions, effects, templates.
- **How to use it:** an app for phone or computer; import clips, cut, add text and audio, export in 9:16.
- **Practical example:** a 30-second video with animated captions in 20 minutes.
- **Pros:** ease of use, automatic captions, a rich free version.
- **Cons:** some advanced and AI features only on paid plans; prices differ by country; read the content terms of use.
- **Prices:** free; Pro with variable prices (sources range from about $8 to $20/month: check).
- **Free alternative:** DaVinci Resolve, the social apps' built-in editors.

### Descript

- **What it's for:** editing video and podcasts by editing the transcript like a text document.
- **Practical example:** you delete sentences from the transcript and the video is cut accordingly; you remove "ums" and pauses.
- **Pros:** very fast for talking-head videos and podcasts.
- **Cons:** less suited to highly creative edits; sources report prices differently.
- **Prices:** limited free plan; paid plans (about $16–24/month according to third-party sources; check).
- **Free alternative:** CapCut.

### Canva (photo editing)

- **What it's for:** background removal, retouching, resizing, AI image editing.
- **Pros:** everything in one tool alongside design.
- **Prices:** see "Digital products."

### Photopea and GIMP

- **What they're for:** free advanced photo retouching (Photopea works in the browser, similar to Photoshop).
- **Pros:** free, powerful.
- **Cons:** steeper learning curve.

## Audio and AI voices

### ElevenLabs

- **What it's for:** realistic voiceovers in many languages, dubbing, sound effects, cloning your own voice.
- **How to use it:** paste the text, choose a voice, adjust stability and style, generate and download the audio.
- **Practical example:** an English voiceover for a 30-second promotional video.
- **Pros:** very high quality, many languages.
- **Cons:** credit system; cloning requires the person's consent and responsible use.
- **Prices:** limited free plan; Starter about $6/month; Creator $22/month (third-party sources, November 2026; check).
- **Free alternative:** synthetic voices built into CapCut; your own recorded voice.

### Music generators (for example Suno, Udio)

- **What they're for:** creating music tracks from a description.
- **Warning:** carefully check the plan's **commercial license** and the legal situation: this sector has been the subject of lawsuits with record labels.
- **Safe alternative:** licensed music libraries included in editing tools or social platforms.

### Audio cleanup tools

- **What they're for:** reducing noise and echo in recordings (features available in Descript, CapCut, Adobe Podcast).
- **Practical example:** a recording made in your kitchen that sounds like it was made in a studio.

## Presentations

### Gamma

- **What it's for:** generating presentations, documents and web pages from a description or text.
- **How to use it:** paste an outline or a document, choose a style, edit the generated slides.
- **Practical example:** a 10-slide presentation to pitch a service to a client.
- **Pros:** speed, modern look.
- **Cons:** content must be revised; export and advanced features on paid plans.
- **Prices:** free with credits; paid plans (not verified: check gamma.app).
- **Free alternative:** Google Slides, Canva.

### Canva Presentations, PowerPoint with Copilot, Google Slides with Gemini

- **What they're for:** presentations with AI assistance built into the editor you already use.
- **Pros:** familiarity, collaboration.
- **Prices:** included in the respective subscriptions.

## Automation

### Zapier

- **What it's for:** connecting different apps so that an event in one triggers an action in another, without coding.
- **How to use it:** choose a **trigger** (for example "new form submitted on the website") and one or more **actions** ("add a row to the spreadsheet," "ask AI for a draft reply," "email the manager").
- **Practical example:** every new quote request is saved, classified by AI and notified.
- **Pros:** simple, a huge number of connected apps.
- **Cons:** costs grow with volume.
- **Prices:** free with 100 tasks a month and two-step automations; Professional about $20/month billed annually (check).
- **Free alternative:** Make, n8n.

### Make

- **What it's for:** more complex visual automations with branching scenarios.
- **Prices:** free with 1,000 credits a month and 2 active scenarios (according to public sources); inexpensive paid plans (check).
- **Pros:** powerful and affordable.
- **Cons:** a more complex interface for beginners.

### n8n

- **What it's for:** advanced automations, which can also be installed on your own server.
- **Pros:** open source, control over data.
- **Cons:** requires more technical skill.

## Digital products

### Canva

- **What it's for:** design, documents, presentations, sellable templates, ebooks, mockups, simple videos.
- **Practical example:** a 30-page planner with a cover, interior pages and previews for Etsy.
- **Pros:** very easy; thousands of templates; AI features.
- **Cons:** licenses must be read carefully for resale; less typographic control than professional software.
- **Prices:** free; Pro $144/year for one person (November 2026, after a price reduction; third-party sources) or about $18/month.
- **Free alternative:** the free plan, Google Slides, Figma.

### Notion

- **What it's for:** workspaces, databases, sellable templates.
- **Prices:** free plan for individual use; paid plans with AI (check).

### What we learned
- For each category, one or two well-known tools are enough.
- Almost every tool has a free plan good enough to try it.
- Prices and terms change often: always check the official pages.
- Commercial licenses and data handling are as important as quality when choosing.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Subscribing to many tools without a specific task.
- Ignoring commercial licenses (images, music, templates).
- Forgetting about unused active subscriptions.
- Choosing tools only because they're "trendy."

### Practical exercise
Fill in a table with the tools you'll use for your chosen business: task, tool, plan, monthly cost, commercial license checked (yes/no), data handled. Set a monthly reminder to review your subscriptions.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've chosen at most 4–5 tools to start.
- [ ] I've checked current prices on the official websites.
- [ ] I've read the commercial terms of use.
- [ ] I know where the data I enter goes.
- [ ] I have a reminder to review my subscriptions.
:::
