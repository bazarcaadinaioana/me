# Complete Tutorials {.part}

This part contains five simulated projects, guided step by step from idea to launch. They differ from the chapters in Part 2: there you saw *what* the business models are; here you'll see *how to carry them out*, with the prompts, files, checks and deadlines. The projects are hypothetical, but every step can be replicated with real tools.

# Project 1: Creating an Ebook and Selling It on Etsy {.chapter}

## The project at a glance

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Product | "The Moving Workbook": a printable PDF ebook-workbook with an 8-week plan, checklists and fill-in tables |
| Audience | Families moving house in Italy |
| Why it suits Etsy | It's a **designed** product (structure, layout, fill-in tables), not an AI-generated "functional guide": the checklists come from the author's experience of three moves and were tested by two families |
| Price | €7.90 |
| Project length | 3 weeks part-time |

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** Since 22 October 2026 Etsy has banned AI-generated functional guides (instructions, recipes, patterns). This project is set up so that the informational content is written and verified by the author, while AI is used for organization, layout and revision. Reread the current rules before publishing and, if your product is mainly informational, consider other platforms (Payhip, Gumroad, your own website).
:::

## Phase 1 — Research (days 1–3)

1. On Etsy, search for "moving planner," "moving checklist," "house move printable": note the automatic suggestions.
2. Open 15 competing listings. Record: price, language, number of pages, format, reviews, complaints.
3. A typical finding: many planners are in English, use American formats (Letter) and references that aren't relevant to Italy (services, documents).
4. **Differentiating angle:** in Italian, A4 format, with the typical paperwork of a house move in Italy (transferring utilities, changing your registered residence, bulky waste collection) to be checked on your own municipality's website.

**Analysis prompt:**

```text
I'm pasting the descriptions and reviews of 15 moving planners sold
online. List: 1) the features almost all of them have; 2) recurring
complaints with verbatim quotes; 3) 5 features that are missing and
that a planner for Italian families should have.
```

## Phase 2 — Design (days 4–6)

1. Write the outline by hand, starting from your experience; then ask AI to reorganize it and point out gaps.
2. Final structure (32 pages):
   - how to use the workbook;
   - an 8-week countdown calendar;
   - weekly checklists;
   - room-by-room inventory;
   - a table for comparing moving company quotes;
   - a utilities and paperwork table (with space to note the deadlines of your own municipality and providers);
   - printable box labels;
   - a "first day in your new home" page.
3. Write the informational content yourself; use AI to harmonize the tone and proofread.

## Phase 3 — Layout (days 7–11)

1. In Canva, create a vertical A4 document.
2. Define 2 colors, 2 fonts, one icon style.
3. Create a template for each type of page, then duplicate.
4. Make the PDF **fillable or printable** (state which in the description).
5. Export as a "Print" PDF (high quality) and check by printing 3 test pages.

## Phase 4 — Listing images (days 12–13)

Prepare 7 images in 4:3 landscape format (check Etsy's recommended sizes):

1. the cover, with a title readable as a thumbnail;
2. an overview of 6 interior pages;
3. a close-up of the 8-week calendar;
4. box labels;
5. "What you get": number of pages, format, files;
6. an example of a page filled in by hand (a real photo);
7. printing instructions.

## Phase 5 — The listing (day 14)

**Prompt for title and tags:**

```text
Act as an Etsy SEO expert for the Italian market.
Product: [description]. Audience: Italian families moving house.
Write: 1) 5 titles of up to 140 characters, clear for people,
with the most important keywords at the start; 2) 13 tags of up to
20 characters each, without needlessly repeating words from the
title; 3) a description with: what you get, format, how to use it,
what is NOT included, a note on AI use.
```

AI-use note to include in the description (example):

> *The content and checklists were written and verified by the author based on her own experience. Artificial intelligence tools were used to edit the text and for some decorative elements.*

In the listing process, select the correct options for who made the product and for AI use, according to the options Etsy offers.

## Phase 6 — Launch (days 15–21)

1. Publish the listing and 2–3 complementary listings (for example box labels alone at €2.90; a moving + first-week-in-the-new-home bundle).
2. Create 5 vertical Pinterest pins linked to the listing.
3. Share it in your network with a personal message.
4. After 14 days, analyze the statistics and improve the first image of the listing with the fewest clicks.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — the project's economics.**
Net per sale at €7.90: about €6.25 after the listing fee, transaction fee, payment processing (assumption: 4% + €0.30) and VAT on fees (assumption). Project costs: one month of Canva Pro and a few euros in listing fees. Hours worked: about 35. With 10 sales a month (an assumption) the net would be about €62 a month before taxes: a project like this makes sense **as part of a collection**, not on its own.
:::

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- On Etsy, a product designed and verified by you is the right path under the new rules.
- Researching competitors' complaints guides differentiation.
- Images, title, tags and an honest description are part of the product.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Selling AI-generated instructions.
- Formats unsuited to the market (Letter instead of A4).
- Treating a single product priced at a few euros as a complete business.

### Practical exercise
Choose a workbook topic on which you have direct experience. Carry out phases 1 and 2 and prepare the complete outline.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've analyzed 15 competitors.
- [ ] The informational content is my own and verified.
- [ ] I have 7 listing images.
- [ ] I've disclosed AI use correctly.
- [ ] I've planned complementary products.
:::

# Project 2: Creating a Book and Publishing It on Amazon KDP {.chapter}

## The project at a glance

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Book | "Your First Balcony Vegetable Garden: 12 Months of Tasks, Mistakes to Avoid and a Growing Journal" |
| Author | An enthusiast with 6 years of experience on a city balcony (a fictional character) |
| Formats | Kindle ebook + 6 × 9 inch paperback, black-and-white interior, about 140 pages |
| AI disclosure | Text written by the author with AI assistance (no disclosure needed); interior illustrations generated with AI and retouched (disclosure needed) |
| Duration | 8–10 weeks part-time |

## Phase 1 — Research and positioning (week 1)

1. On Amazon, search for "balcony vegetable garden," "container gardening," "vegetable garden for beginners."
2. For the top 20 results, note: price, pages, date, number of reviews, 2–3 star reviews.
3. Typical complaints to look for: "too generic," "written for people with a garden," "beautiful photos but few instructions."
4. Positioning: **balconies only**, a **month-by-month calendar**, a **fill-in journal** (which also justifies the print format).

## Phase 2 — Structure and writing (weeks 2–5)

1. Write the outline (12 monthly chapters + 4 foundation chapters + journal).
2. For each chapter, record a voice note with what you know and do.
3. Transcribe it and ask AI for an organized draft:

```text
I'm giving you the transcript of my notes on March for a balcony
vegetable garden. Turn them into a chapter of about 1,500 words with:
a short introduction, the month's tasks (numbered list), what to sow,
common mistakes, a "My balcony in March" box with my experience.
Don't add information that isn't in the notes; if important
information is missing, list it at the end as questions for me.
```

4. Rewrite it in your own voice, check the horticultural facts against reliable sources (manuals, research institutes, agricultural bodies), add your own photos or diagrams.
5. Have 3 beginners read it.

## Phase 3 — Formatting (weeks 6–7)

1. Download the 6 × 9 Word template from KDP's help section, or use Kindle Create.
2. Set up: margins according to KDP's guidance for the page count, running heads, page numbers, table of contents.
3. For the ebook, generate an EPUB file (Kindle Create, exported Word, or dedicated tools) and check the preview with **Kindle Previewer**.
4. Convert the paperback file to PDF with embedded fonts.

## Phase 4 — Cover (week 7)

1. In KDP's **cover calculator**, enter trim size, page count and paper type; download the template with the measurements for front, spine and back.
2. Create the cover in Canva or with a designer, respecting the safe areas.
3. Back cover: short description, author bio, space for the barcode (KDP adds it automatically).
4. Check the title's readability as a thumbnail (as it appears in Amazon search).

## Phase 5 — Book details (week 8)

**Prompt for the description:**

```text
Write the Amazon description for my book [title, subtitle, summary].
Structure: an opening sentence naming the reader's problem;
3 short paragraphs; a bulleted list "In this book you'll find";
a paragraph "Who this book is for." Maximum 250 words.
Don't use superlatives; don't promise guaranteed results.
```

Then fill in: 7 keywords or phrases (readers' real searches), categories, AI disclosure, territorial rights, price using the calculator.

## Phase 6 — Proof and publication (weeks 8–9)

1. Order a **proof copy** of the paperback.
2. Check: margins, binding, contrast of the illustrations, typos.
3. Correct, re-upload, publish. Amazon's review usually takes up to 72 hours.

## Phase 7 — Launch (week 10)

1. Send complimentary ebook copies to 15 readers in your audience (gardening groups, friends of friends who are enthusiasts), asking for an honest review if they wish, with no conditions.
2. Prepare 6 social posts with tips from the book.
3. Offer a free talk at a library or a local garden center.
4. After a month, consider an Amazon Ads campaign with a minimum daily budget and precise keywords.

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- A solid KDP book comes from real experience, precise positioning and a consistent format.
- AI turns notes and recordings into organized drafts, without adding invented content.
- The cover calculator, Kindle Previewer and a proof copy prevent the most common mistakes.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Letting AI "fill out" chapters with unverified information.
- Skipping the proof copy.
- Asking for reviews in exchange for favors or payment.

### Practical exercise
Record a 10-minute voice note on a topic you know well. Transcribe it and use the Phase 2 prompt to get a chapter draft. Compare the draft with your notes: what was added? What's missing?

### Review checklist
- [ ] I have precise positioning relative to competitors.
- [ ] Every chapter comes from my notes and is verified.
- [ ] The cover and interior meet KDP's measurements.
- [ ] I've completed the AI disclosure correctly.
- [ ] I've ordered a proof copy.
:::

# Project 3: Building an Instagram Management Service with AI {.chapter}

## The project at a glance

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Service | Instagram management for beauty salons and wellness centers |
| Offer | 12 pieces of content a month (6 carousels, 4 reels, 2 posts), weekly stories, monthly report |
| Price | €550/month, minimum 3-month contract |
| Project length | 4 weeks to prepare the service and close the first client |

## Phase 1 — Set up the system (week 1)

Create this structure in Google Drive (or similar) for each client:

```text
Client-SalonName/
├── 00-Brief and contract/
├── 01-Identity (logo, colors, fonts, tone)/
├── 02-Real photos and videos/
├── 03-Editorial plans/
├── 04-Content to approve/
├── 05-Approved content/
└── 06-Reports/
```

Prepare the basic documents:

1. **Brief questionnaire** (services, publishable prices, typical clients, competitors, things never to say, goals).
2. **Contract** (scope, number of pieces, revisions, approval times, ownership of materials, duration, price, ad budget excluded). Have it checked by a professional.
3. **Editorial plan template** (date, format, pillar, hook, copy, visual, status).
4. **Report template** (reach, engagement, saves, profile visits, clicks, messages, 3 observations, 3 actions for next month).

## Phase 2 — Set up AI for the client (week 1)

Create a **project** in your AI assistant with these instructions:

```text
You are the communications assistant for [Salon], a beauty salon
in [city]. Audience: women aged 25-55 who care about self-care and
have little free time.
Pillars: 1) treatments explained; 2) at-home routine tips;
3) behind the scenes and the team; 4) approved news and promotions.
Tone: warm, professional, never judgmental about bodies.
Rules: no promises of medical or guaranteed results; no
"before and after" without the client's written consent; no price
not in the attached price list; don't mention product brands that
aren't in the price list.
Attachments: price list, FAQ, 10 approved posts as a style reference.
```

## Phase 3 — Find the first client (weeks 2–3)

1. List 30 salons in your area; analyze their profiles.
2. Choose the 10 with potential (active but inconsistent, beautiful photos but weak copy, or vice versa).
3. For each, prepare a one-page **mini-audit** and a sample carousel already laid out in their style.
4. Visit or write in a personalized way; suggest a 20-minute call.
5. On the call: listen to their goals, show the audit, present the offer, propose starting next month.

## Phase 4 — Onboarding (week 4)

1. Contract signed and first invoice.
2. Access to the profile through Meta's official tools (professional account, roles in Meta Business Suite): don't ask for the personal password.
3. Complete brief and a session of real photos and videos in the salon (2 hours).
4. The first month's editorial plan for approval.

## Phase 5 — Monthly production

**Prompt for the editorial plan:**

```text
Create the editorial plan for [month] for [Salon]: 12 pieces
(6 carousels, 4 reels, 2 posts). Spread the 4 pillars. Consider
the month's occasions relevant to the sector. For each piece
state: proposed date, format, pillar, hook (max 8 words),
content summary, real photos/videos needed, call to action.
Present the result as a table.
```

**Prompt for a reel:**

```text
Write the script for a 25-second reel about the [name] treatment.
Structure: a spoken hook in the first 2 seconds; 3 scenes with
directions on what to film in the salon; on-screen text for each
scene (max 6 words); a caption of 80-120 words; 5 relevant
hashtags specific to the city. Follow the project's rules.
```

Workflow: plan → approval → production (copy with AI + review; graphics in Canva with the brand kit; videos from real footage) → approval → scheduling in Meta Business Suite → publication → report.

## Phase 6 — Report and renewal

At the end of the month export the data and ask AI for a first reading, then write the conclusions yourself. Present the report in a 15-minute call: results, what worked, what you'll change.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — time per client.**
Brief and plan: 3 hours; filming: 2 hours; production: 10–12 hours; scheduling and management: 2 hours; report: 2 hours. About 20 hours a month in total. At €550/month, about €27 gross an hour in the first months; with established processes and templates the time tends to decrease. Taxes, social contributions and tool costs must be subtracted.
:::

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- A professional service is built on folders, documents and processes defined before the first client.
- One AI project per client with clear rules ensures consistency and safety.
- A personalized mini-audit is the most effective sales tool.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Asking for personal passwords instead of using official roles.
- Publishing without approval.
- Using "before and after" without consent or promising aesthetic results.

### Practical exercise
Prepare the complete kit: folder structure, brief questionnaire, editorial plan template, report template, AI project instructions for a fictional client in the sector you prefer.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I have the folder structure and the 4 basic documents.
- [ ] I have a checked contract.
- [ ] I have AI project instructions with the client's rules.
- [ ] I've prepared at least 3 mini-audits.
:::

# Project 4: Creating a Digital Product and Promoting It on Social Media {.chapter}

## The project at a glance

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Product | "Quote kit for tradespeople": a spreadsheet for quotes and price lists + 5 customer message templates + a PDF guide |
| Audience | Tradespeople and small building firms (plumbers, electricians, painters) |
| Price | €29 (€19 launch price for 7 days, clearly stated) |
| Channels | Instagram and TikTok with short videos + professional groups + email |
| Platform | Payhip |
| Duration | 5 weeks |

## Phase 1 — Validation (week 1)

1. Interview 5 tradespeople: how do they do quotes today? How long does it take? What do they forget?
2. Typical result to verify: quotes on paper or via WhatsApp, forgotten line items, no history, difficulty following up with customers who don't reply.
3. Promise: "Clear quotes in 10 minutes from your phone, without forgetting a single item."

## Phase 2 — Building (weeks 2–3)

1. Design the spreadsheet with AI:

```text
Design a Google Sheet for a tradesperson who needs to write quotes.
Sheets: Price list (item, unit, price, category), Quote (customer,
items from a drop-down menu, quantity, total, configurable VAT,
notes), History (date, customer, amount, status: sent/accepted/
declined, follow-up date). Specify columns, exact formulas, data
validation and conditional formatting for quotes to follow up on.
```

2. Build the file and test it with 3 real quotes provided by the tradespeople you interviewed (anonymized).
3. Write the 5 message templates (sending the quote, polite reminder, job confirmation, deposit request, review request) and the PDF guide with screenshots.
4. Have 2 tradespeople try the kit for a week.

::: {.box .tip}
**Tip.** In the spreadsheet, leave the VAT rate **configurable** and state in the guide that rates and invoicing rules must be checked with your own accountant. Don't give tax advice in a product.
:::

## Phase 3 — Sales page and store (week 3)

1. Create the product on Payhip: file (or a link to copy the sheet), images, description, price.
2. A sales page with the 9 elements (Part 4): result, who it's for, contents, previews, author, price, FAQ, legal information, button.
3. Create a **free resource**: "Checklist of the 15 items tradespeople forget in quotes" in exchange for an email.
4. Prepare a sequence of 4 emails: delivering the checklist → a practical tip → presenting the kit with the launch offer → the last day of the offer.

## Phase 4 — Social content (weeks 3–5)

**Pillars:**

1. mistakes in quotes;
2. how to communicate with customers;
3. behind the scenes of the kit;
4. tradespeople's frequently asked questions.

**Prompt for 15 video ideas:**

```text
Generate 15 ideas for 20-30 second videos for Italian tradespeople
on quotes and customer relationships. For each idea: spoken hook
(max 10 words), 3 content points, on-screen text, a closing
invitation to download the free checklist. Direct, concrete tone,
no marketing jargon. Avoid income promises.
```

**Calendar:** 3 videos a week for 3 weeks; every video invites viewers to the free checklist (link in profile).

Record with your phone, edit in CapCut with captions. If you use an AI voice, consider the platform's AI content label.

## Phase 5 — Launch (week 5)

1. Day 1: email to subscribers with the 7-day launch offer (real price and deadline).
2. Days 1–7: one video a day showing the kit in use.
3. Professional groups: a useful (not promotional) post with a tip and, where the group's rules allow, the link to the checklist.
4. Day 7: last-day email.
5. Day 8: the price returns to €29. Collect feedback from the first buyers.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — launch.**
Let's assume 120 list subscribers in 3 weeks and a 5% purchase rate during the launch (an assumption): 6 sales at €19 = €114 gross, minus the Payhip fee (5%) and payment processing. The most important result is the system: an email list, content and a validated product, which can keep working as new content is added.
:::

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- Interviews and tests with real users before launch reduce risk.
- A free resource + email sequence + social content form a sales system.
- Launch offers, yes, but with real prices and deadlines.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Including tax advice in the product.
- Launching without an email list.
- Videos that sell instead of help.

### Practical exercise
Choose a trade and design with AI a spreadsheet that solves one of its problems. Build it and test it with realistic data.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've interviewed at least 5 potential users.
- [ ] At least 2 people have tried the product.
- [ ] I have a free resource, email sequence and sales page.
- [ ] I have at least 9 videos ready for the launch.
:::

# Project 5: Building a Small AI Services Business for Companies {.chapter}

## The project at a glance

| Item | Choice |
|---|---|
| Business | AI services for independent accommodation businesses (small hotels, B&Bs, farm stays) |
| Services | 1) "AI for the front desk" workshop; 2) Replies and reviews kit; 3) Automating availability requests |
| Prices | Workshop €450; kit €600; automation €1,200; support €180/month |
| Duration | 6–8 weeks to launch and close the first project |

## Phase 1 — Expertise and demo case (weeks 1–2)

1. Study the sector: what are a front desk's repetitive tasks? (Replies to availability requests, information on parking and hours, replies to reviews, pre-arrival messages, local recommendations.)
2. Create a **demo case** with a fictional property:
   - a "property knowledge" document (services, hours, rules, area);
   - an AI assistant configured with that document;
   - 20 typical guest questions and a check of the answers;
   - reply templates for reviews (positive, mixed, negative).
3. Measure: how long it takes to answer 10 typical messages with and without the system.

## Phase 2 — Offer and documents (week 3)

Describe each service with: result, what's included, what isn't, timelines, price, what you need from the client.

**Example — Replies and reviews kit (€600):**

- a 1-hour interview with the owner;
- the property knowledge document;
- setting up an assistant on a plan suitable for business use, chosen with the client;
- 40 reply templates;
- a 6-page user guide for staff with the rules (what not to enter, check before sending);
- 1 training session of 90 minutes;
- 30 days of email support.

Documents: proposal, contract, data processing agreement if you'll access guests' personal data, user guide.

## Phase 3 — Client acquisition (weeks 3–5)

1. List 40 properties in your province; look at how they reply to online reviews (timing, tone, missing replies).
2. Contact the local tourism consortium or association offering a **free 45-minute session**, "Practical AI for small properties."
3. At the session: a live demo with the demo case, attention to risks (privacy, errors), collecting the contact details of those interested.
4. Offer 3 interested people a **free 45-minute analysis**.

**Prompt to prepare the analysis:**

```text
Act as a process consultant for small accommodation businesses.
Here's how the front desk at [property] works: [description].
Identify the 5 tasks with the most potential for time savings
thanks to AI. For each: description of the solution, possible tools,
risks (privacy, errors, tone), estimated time saved per week with
the assumptions used, priority. State clearly that the estimates
are assumptions to be verified with a trial period.
```

## Phase 4 — Pilot project (weeks 5–7)

1. The client chooses the kit (€600).
2. Interview, knowledge document, setup, testing with 30 real past questions (anonymized).
3. Staff training: how to use it, what not to do, how to check.
4. Two weeks of trial, measuring response times.

## Phase 5 — Wrap-up and growth (week 8)

1. Pilot report: times before and after (measured), problems encountered, improvements.
2. A proposal for monthly support (updating the knowledge document, new templates, quality control).
3. With the client's consent, create a documented case with real data for your communications.
4. A proposal for the next service (automating availability requests).

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — first quarter.**
2 workshops (€900), 2 kits (€1,200), 1 client on support for 2 months (€360): revenue €2,460. Costs: tools for testing and demos, travel, professional liability insurance, an accountant. Estimated total time: 80–100 hours, a large part of it spent on acquisition. Taxes and social contributions according to the tax regime.
:::

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** An assistant that replies to guests can get hours, prices or rules wrong. Always set up a **human check** before sending for replies containing binding information (prices, availability, cancellation terms), and update the knowledge document whenever something changes.
:::

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- A small AI agency starts from a precise sector and a measured demo case.
- Workshops with associations and free analyses generate the first projects.
- A pilot, measurement and a report turn a project into a recurring client.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Promising unmeasured time savings.
- Setting up assistants without human-check rules.
- Handling guests' data without agreements and appropriate plans.

### Practical exercise
Choose a sector and build the demo case: knowledge document, 20 typical questions, a check of the answers, time measurement.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I have a demo case with measured times.
- [ ] I have three services described with fixed prices.
- [ ] I have a proposal, contract and data agreement.
- [ ] I have a human-check rule for binding information.
:::
