# Publishing Books on Amazon KDP {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

**Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP)** is Amazon's self-publishing platform. It lets you publish Kindle ebooks, paperbacks and, in some markets, hardcovers for free. Amazon prints the book only when someone orders it (*print on demand*), ships it and pays you a **royalty**, a percentage of the price.

You pay nothing to publish: Amazon keeps its share of every copy sold. This makes KDP accessible, but also extremely crowded: a huge number of new titles are published every day, many of them mass-produced and of low quality.

## KDP's rules on AI

When you publish, KDP asks you to state whether the book contains **AI-generated content**:

- **AI-generated content** (text, images or translations created by an AI tool) must be **disclosed**, even if you've edited it substantially.
- **AI-assisted content** (you wrote it and used AI to edit, improve or brainstorm) does **not** need to be disclosed.

KDP also limits the number of titles that can be published in a day and removes content that offers a poor reader experience (for example books that summarize or copy other books, content without value, misleading titles). The precise rules are in the "Content Guidelines" section of the KDP Help pages.

## Why someone would pay

On Amazon, people look for books to solve problems, learn, relax or give as gifts. They buy when the title, cover, reviews and preview ("Look inside") convince them. The categories that work best for people starting without an audience are **niche** ones with readers who actively search: specific practical guides, workbooks, activity books with curated content, genre fiction with strong readerships (with real writing work).

## Royalties at a glance (checked November 2026)

| Format | Royalty | Main conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Ebook – 35% option | 35% of list price | No delivery costs |
| Ebook – 70% option | 70% of list price minus **delivery costs** (based on the file's megabytes) | Price within the range set by Amazon for each marketplace; price at least 20% below the print edition; not available for content that is primarily public domain |
| Paperback | A percentage of list price minus **printing costs** | The percentage depends on price and marketplace; since 2025 a lower percentage applies to books under a certain price threshold |

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** The price ranges for 70%, delivery rates and print percentages vary by marketplace and change over time; public sources don't always agree either. **Always** use the royalty calculator inside KDP before setting your price.
:::

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time for your first book | 1–3 months part-time for a quality book |
| Amazon content review | Usually up to 72 hours |
| Skills required | Writing or curation, formatting, cover care, keyword research |
| Initial investment | €0 to publish; €30–150 recommended for proof copies, tools and design; optional advertising budget |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Create your KDP account** at kdp.amazon.com and complete the **tax information** (tax interview) and bank details. Without these steps you won't receive payments.
2. **Research the niche.** Type keywords for your topic into Amazon's search and look at the suggestions. Open the top 20 results: number of reviews, publication date, sales rank, content of 2–3 star reviews.
3. **Define the book**: reader, promise, format (ebook, paperback or both), length, page count.
4. **Write or curate the content.** Use AI as an assistant (outline, drafts, revision), but add real experience, examples and exercises. Remember the disclosure if you use generated content.
5. **Format it.** For the paperback, choose the trim size (for example 6 × 9 inches, i.e. 15.24 × 22.86 cm, very common for nonfiction), margins according to KDP's guidance, page numbers, table of contents. For the ebook, prepare an EPUB file or use Kindle Create.
6. **Create the cover.** The paperback needs a full cover (front, spine, back) with measurements calculated by KDP's **cover calculator** based on page count and paper type.
7. **Fill in the book details**: title, subtitle, author, description, 7 keywords (or phrases), categories, reading age if relevant, AI disclosure.
8. **Set the price** with the royalty calculator.
9. **Order a proof copy** of the paperback before final publication: check margins, colors, readability.
10. **Publish** and check the product page in the various marketplaces.

## How to find your first readers

- **Amazon's internal search:** title, subtitle, keywords and categories determine where you appear.
- **First honest reviews:** send complimentary copies only to readers in your target audience, asking for an honest review without conditioning it. Amazon has strict review rules: don't ask close family members, don't swap reviews, don't pay.
- **Your own audience:** newsletter, social media, topic groups, blog.
- **Amazon Ads:** cost-per-click campaigns you can start with low daily budgets. Think carefully: it's easy to spend more than you earn if the book doesn't convert.

## How to set prices

- **Ebook:** compare the prices of the 20 most relevant titles. Many practical-guide ebooks sit in the range that qualifies for 70%.
- **Paperback:** the price must cover printing costs and leave a margin. Printing costs increase with page count and color: a color book costs much more to print.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — a simplified royalty calculation.**
Ebook at €4.99 on the 70% option, 2 MB file. Let's assume a delivery cost of €0.12 per MB (check your marketplace's rate): €0.24. Royalty ≈ 70% × (€4.99 net of the VAT applied by Amazon − delivery cost). On Amazon.it the price includes VAT on ebooks (reduced to 4% in Italy), so the base is about €4.80: royalty ≈ 70% × (4.80 − 0.24) ≈ **€3.19** per copy, before income tax.
The real calculation is provided by KDP's calculator: use this example only to understand the logic (price → VAT → costs → percentage).
:::

## How to make your first sale

1. Before publishing, line up 10–20 target readers willing to read an advance copy.
2. Publish the ebook and paperback at the same time: a page with multiple formats looks more professional.
3. Announce the release on your channels with a useful excerpt.
4. In the first week, check that the page, categories and preview are correct.

## Complete practical example

**Paolo** (a fictional character) is a swimming instructor with fifteen years of experience with children who are afraid of water.

1. **Book:** "Afraid of the Water? A Parents' Guide: 30 Games to Prepare Children Aged 3 to 6 for Their First Swimming Lesson."
2. **Original content:** the games are ones he has used for years; AI helps him organize the material, write introductions and edit; safety advice is checked against sports federation guidelines.
3. **Disclosure:** the text is written by him with AI assistance for revision (no disclosure needed); the interior illustrations are AI-generated and then retouched (disclosure needed).
4. **Formats:** ebook and 6 × 9 inch paperback, 96 pages, black-and-white interior.
5. **Promotion:** a flyer with a QR code at the pool where he works, parenting groups, preschools.

| 3 | Research 3 niches; analyze 20 titles for each | Competitor analysis |

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- KDP lets you publish ebooks and paperbacks for free, but the market is very crowded.
- AI-generated content must be disclosed; AI-assisted content need not be.
- Royalties depend on price, VAT, delivery and printing costs: use the official calculator.
- Specific niches and real experience are the most solid path.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Publishing mass-produced books without value.
- Failing to disclose AI use when required.
- Covers that are unreadable as thumbnails or have wrong measurements.
- Getting reviews improperly.
- Running ad campaigns without measuring the return.

### Practical exercise
Choose three topics you're an expert in. For each, find the 10 most relevant books on Amazon and read the 2- and 3-star reviews. For each topic, write what readers complain about most often and how your book could solve it.

# Creating Content for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

Creating content means building an **audience** on one or more social platforms by regularly publishing videos, carousels, posts or long-form videos on a topic. That audience, in turn, can generate income in several ways:

- **Platform programs** (ad revenue sharing, bonuses, fan subscriptions), available only after meeting minimum requirements;
- **Brand collaborations** (sponsored content);
- **Affiliate marketing** (commissions on sales of products you recommend);
- **Selling your own products** (ebooks, templates, courses, services): often the most solid source.

AI helps you find ideas, write scripts, generate captions, edit, create thumbnails and analyze results. But platforms reward what people watch to the end, save and share: **personality, usefulness and consistency** matter more than technique.

## Why someone would pay

Here the people who "pay" are mainly brands and customers who trust you thanks to your content. A brand pays a creator because they reach a specific audience with credibility. A customer buys a product from a creator because they've already received free value from them.

## Tools

| Stage | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Ideas and scripts | Free AI assistants | AI assistants, Pro plans |
| Filming | Your smartphone | Clip-on microphone (€20–60), LED light |
| Editing | CapCut (free version), Instagram and TikTok built-in editors, DaVinci Resolve | CapCut Pro, Descript, Adobe Premiere |
| Captions | Automatic captions in the apps | Advanced transcription tools |
| Graphics and thumbnails | Canva | Canva Pro |
| AI voices | Limited free plans | ElevenLabs (Starter about $6/month as of November 2026) |
| Scheduling | Meta Business Suite, YouTube Studio (free) | Multi-platform scheduling tools |

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to first results | Months of consistent publishing; there's no guaranteed timeframe |
| Typical commitment | 5–10 hours a week for 3–5 short pieces |
| Skills | Speaking clearly, choosing topics, basic editing, consistency |
| Initial investment | €0–100 (microphone, light, optional subscription) |

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** Platform monetization programs have requirements (follower counts, watch hours, age, country) that change often. Read the official terms in the apps' "Monetization" sections. Don't count on these programs as your first source of income.
:::

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Choose a topic and an audience** where what you know, what you enjoy and what people search for intersect.
2. **Define 3–4 content "pillars"** (for example, for an account about budget cooking: recipes under €5, organizing the grocery shopping, common mistakes, food storage).
3. **Study 10 accounts in your niche**: which content gets the most views relative to followers? What structure do they use in the first 3 seconds?
4. **Use AI for an idea bank**: 50 ideas per pillar, then choose the best.
5. **Write short scripts** with a structure: hook (first 1–3 seconds), promise, content, close with a call to action (save, follow, comment).
6. **Film in batches**: one afternoon filming several videos reduces total time.
7. **Edit**, add captions (many people watch without sound), check readability on a phone screen.
8. **Publish on a calendar** that's consistent and realistic.
9. **Analyze every week**: average watch time, saves, shares, new followers per piece of content.

::: {.box .tip}
**Tip.** If you use AI-generated voices, faces or videos that depict realistic people, disclose it with the labels the platforms provide (Instagram, TikTok and YouTube have tools for labeling AI content). In the EU, the AI Act requires deepfakes to be recognizable as such. Transparency also protects your reputation.
:::

## How to find your first customers

For a creator, "customers" come in two kinds:

1. **The audience that buys your products:** from the start, build a way to collect contacts (email list, group, channel) and offer a simple first product once you have an audience that engages, even a small one.
2. **Brands:** prepare a **media kit** (who you are, your audience, real statistics, content examples, services and prices) and pitch brands consistent with your topic, even small ones. Sponsored collaborations must always be labeled as advertising, according to the platforms' rules and advertising law (in Italy, also the AGCOM guidelines for influencers above certain thresholds).

## How to set prices

There's no official price list for collaborations. A prudent method:

- consider the **time** needed (ideas, filming, editing, revisions);
- consider the **value** to the brand (audience reached, quality of engagement, usage rights to the content);
- define a **minimum price** below which you won't work;
- charge separately for **usage rights** if the brand wants to use your video in its own ads.

## How to make your first sale

The most likely first income for a small account is a **low-priced product of your own** or a **service** linked to your topic (a consultation, a personalized plan). Example: an account about home organization can sell a €7 printable planner to email subscribers.

## Complete practical example

**Sara** (a fictional character) is a nurse with a passion for healthy, budget-friendly cooking.

1. **Topic:** "Healthy packed lunches for work, under €3."
2. **Pillars:** recipes, preparing the week in an hour, shopping list, storage mistakes.
3. **AI use:** idea bank, seasonal variations, scripts, an indicative cost calculation for recipes (then checked against receipts), captions, thumbnails.
4. **Rhythm:** 3 short videos a week on Instagram and TikTok, 1 long video a month on YouTube.
5. **Gradual monetization:** an email list with a free seasonal shopping list → a recipe ebook **written and tested by her** (not generated) at €9 → collaborations with food-container brands.

## Action plan for the first 7 days

| Day | Activity | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose topic, audience and 3–4 pillars | Positioning |
| 2 | Analyze 10 accounts and note 20 effective hooks | Research |
| 3 | Generate and select 30 ideas with AI | Idea bank |
| 4 | Write 6 scripts | Scripts ready |
| 5 | Film 6 videos in one session | Footage |
| 6 | Edit, caption, prepare covers | 6 videos ready |
| 7 | Publish the first video and schedule the others | Calendar running |

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- An audience is an asset that can be monetized in several ways, especially with your own products and services.
- AI speeds up ideas, scripts and editing, but success depends on usefulness, personality and consistency.
- Platform programs have changing requirements: they're not a reliable base to start from.
- Advertising and realistic AI content must always be disclosed.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Publishing inconsistently or constantly changing topic.
- Copying other creators' content.
- Buying followers or engagement.
- Not collecting your own contacts and relying only on the algorithm.
- Not labeling paid collaborations.

### Practical exercise
Choose a topic and write 5 different hooks for the same video (a question, a common mistake, a number, a before/after, a surprising but true statement). Record the 5 versions, publish two of them on different days and compare average watch time.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've defined my topic, audience and pillars.
- [ ] I have an idea bank of at least 30 ideas.
- [ ] I have a realistic publishing calendar.
- [ ] I have a system for collecting contacts.
- [ ] I know how to label AI and sponsored content.
:::

# Managing Social Media for Businesses and Professionals {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

**Social media management** is the service of running a business's or professional's social media presence: strategy, editorial plan, content creation, publishing, replying to comments (if agreed), analyzing results. The client pays a monthly fee.

It's one of the activities where AI has the strongest impact on productivity: what used to take days (ideas, copy, graphics, reports) now takes much less time. This lets you serve more clients or offer a more polished service at the same price.

## Why someone would pay

Many small business owners know they need to be on social media but lack the time, ideas or consistency. They pay to:

- **take a recurring worry off their plate**;
- **look professional** and consistent;
- **get measurable results**: inquiries, bookings, store visits;
- have **someone accountable** for the results.

## Tools

| Stage | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy and copy | Free AI assistants | AI assistants, Pro plans with "projects" for each client |
| Graphics | Free Canva | Canva Pro (brand kit, resizing, scheduling) |
| Short videos | CapCut, in-app editors | CapCut Pro, Descript |
| Scheduling | Meta Business Suite (Facebook and Instagram) | Multi-platform tools |
| Approvals and organization | Free Google Drive, Trello, Notion | Paid plans of the same |
| Reports | Native platform analytics, Google Looker Studio | Reporting tools |

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to get started | 2–4 weeks to prepare your offer, portfolio and first contacts |
| Skills | Communication, organization, knowledge of the platforms, client management |
| Investment | €0–50/month (Canva Pro, AI assistant) |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Choose a sector** (restaurants, medical practices, gyms, clothing stores, professionals): specializing makes it easier to find clients and create effective content.
2. **Create a demo portfolio.** If you have no clients, create 3 "case studies" for fictional businesses or, with their consent, redo for free the strategy of a business you know. Always state that they're examples.
3. **Define your packages** (see the pricing table).
4. **Prepare the documents**: an onboarding questionnaire for the client, a contract (have it checked by a professional), a content approval process, a monthly report template.
5. **Build your production system with AI**: one project per client with instructions, tone, products, audience and approved content.
6. **Set a monthly workflow**:
   - week 1: next month's editorial plan and approval;
   - week 2: production of copy, graphics and videos;
   - week 3: scheduling;
   - week 4: analysis and report.

## How to find your first clients

- **Local contacts:** visit businesses in your sector and area in person with a concrete example (for example 3 posts already made for them).
- **A short free audit:** analyze a business's profile and send 3 concrete suggestions. Don't criticize: propose.
- **A network of professionals:** accountants, photographers, web designers and print shops can refer clients to you.
- **Your own profile:** publish case studies and tips for your sector.

## How to set prices

| Package | Typical content | Indicative range in Italy |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 8 posts/month on 1 platform, scheduling, short report | €250–450/month |
| Standard | 12 pieces/month (posts + short videos), 2 platforms, report | €450–800/month |
| Complete | 16–20 pieces, stories, comment management, ad campaigns (budget excluded) | €800–1,500+/month |

These ranges are indicative, depend on the city, sector and experience, and come from observing market offers, not from official statistics. Always make clear that the **advertising budget** is separate from your fee and that **professional on-site photos and videos** may cost extra.

::: {.box .tip}
**Tip.** Don't sell "posts"; sell **agreed, measurable results**: more booking requests, more messages, more website visits. But don't promise guaranteed numbers: promise a method, a frequency and a transparent report.
:::

## How to make your first sale

1. Offer a **trial month** at a reduced price with clear goals and a review meeting at the end.
2. Prepare a presentation with: analysis of the current profile, 3 concrete ideas, a sample 2-week editorial plan, price, next steps.
3. Close with a simple question: "Shall we start on the first of the month?"

## Complete practical example

**Elena** (a fictional character) used to work as a sales assistant in a clothing store and knows the local fashion sector well.

1. **Specialization:** independent clothing boutiques.
2. **Offer:** Standard package: 12 pieces a month (6 carousels, 4 short videos, 2 new-arrival posts), weekly stories with new arrivals photographed in store, a monthly report.
3. **AI use:** editorial plan, copy, video ideas based on trends, coordinated graphics, first-pass data analysis. Product photos are **always real**: AI never alters garments or invents products.
4. **Process:** every Monday Elena spends 2 hours in the store taking photos and videos; she produces the rest from home.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — Elena's numbers.**
Three clients at €600/month = €1,800 a month in revenue. Costs: Canva Pro, AI assistant, travel (assumption: €80 a month). Time: about 20–25 hours a month per client at first, decreasing with experience. Taxes and social contributions must be calculated on this revenue according to the tax regime (see Part 8). Finding three clients can take months of outreach: factor this time into your plans.
:::

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- Social media management is a service with recurring monthly income and widespread demand.
- AI greatly reduces production time: specialization and process make the difference.
- Package pricing, a separate ad budget, no promises of guaranteed results.
- First clients most often come from direct local contact with concrete examples.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Accepting any client without specializing.
- Working without a contract or approval process.
- Using AI images that alter the client's real products.
- Promising guaranteed followers or sales.
- Prices so low you have to take on too many clients.

### Practical exercise
Choose a real business in your area. Analyze its Instagram profile and write a one-page audit: what works, what to improve, 3 concrete content ideas with ready-to-use copy. Don't send it yet: use it as the first piece of your portfolio.
