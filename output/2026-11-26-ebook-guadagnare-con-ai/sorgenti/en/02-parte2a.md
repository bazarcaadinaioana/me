# 12 Ways to Make Money with AI {.part}

This part analyzes twelve business models using the same framework, so you can compare them honestly. For each one you'll find: what it is, why someone would pay, free and paid tools, timeframes, skills, investment, procedure, first clients, pricing, first sale, a complete example, a 7-day plan and, in the final summary, mistakes to avoid, an exercise and a checklist.

All financial examples are **hypothetical** and are there to show how to do the math. Platform fees are those published in November 2026; always check them before you start.

# Creating and Selling Ebooks {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

An **ebook** is a digital book, usually in PDF or EPUB format, that the customer downloads and reads on a computer, tablet, phone or e-reader. In this chapter we're talking about ebooks sold **directly by you**: on your website, through platforms such as Gumroad, Payhip or Lemon Squeezy, or through social media. Publishing on Amazon and selling on Etsy each have their own chapter, because they follow specific rules.

It works simply: you create the file once, upload it to a sales platform, and every time someone pays they automatically receive the download link. AI helps you with research, structure, drafting, revision, the cover and promotion. It does **not** replace your expertise on the subject: an ebook is valuable if it contains useful knowledge, organized better than what the reader could find for free.

## Why someone would pay

Almost everything can be found free on the internet. People pay for an ebook when it offers at least one of these benefits:

- **Time saved:** it gathers into an orderly path what is scattered elsewhere.
- **Specificity:** it answers a precise problem for a precise audience ("weekly menus for families with a celiac child," not "healthy recipes").
- **Trust:** it's written by someone with demonstrable experience in the subject.
- **Applicability:** it contains frameworks, checklists, examples and exercises to use right away.

The best-selling ebooks aren't the ones "about a topic," but the ones that take the reader **from situation A to situation B**.

## Tools

| Stage | Free tools | Paid tools |
|---|---|---|
| Research and structure | ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude (free plans), Perplexity | The same in Pro versions (~$20/month) |
| Writing and revision | Google Docs, LibreOffice, AI assistants | An AI assistant subscription |
| Layout | Canva (free plan), Google Docs → PDF | Canva Pro ($144/year as of November 2026), Affinity, Vellum (Mac) |
| Cover and images | Canva, image generators with free plans | Midjourney (from $10/month) |
| Sales | Payhip (free with a 5% fee), Gumroad (10% + $0.50 per direct sale, plus payment costs) | Paid Payhip plans, Stan Store (from $29/month), your own website |
| Email to readers | Free plans of MailerLite, Brevo, Kit | Paid plans above a certain number of contacts |

::: {.box .tip}
**Tip.** For your first ebooks, prefer a platform that acts as **merchant of record**, meaning it sells on your behalf and calculates and pays the VAT owed in different countries. Gumroad, for example, has done this since 2025. This greatly simplifies the tax side of international sales, although it doesn't remove your own reporting obligations.
:::

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time for your first ebook | 3–6 weeks part-time for a quality guide of 40–80 pages |
| Skills required | Real knowledge of the subject, ability to organize, careful revision |
| Skills AI supplements | Structure, first drafts, editing, titles, descriptions, basic design |
| Initial investment | From €0 (free tools) to €50–100 (one month of an AI Pro plan + design) |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Choose a topic in which you have experience, or can verifiably acquire it.** List 10 things you do better than average or problems you've solved for yourself or others.
2. **Define the reader.** Write one sentence: "This ebook is for [who] who wants [result] but is stuck because of [problem]."
3. **Check demand.** Search for the topic on Amazon, in Facebook groups, on Reddit, in YouTube comments. Look for recurring questions and complaints about existing books ("too theoretical," "missing an example of…").
4. **Create the table of contents with AI.** Use a prompt with the reader, the goal and the problems you've collected. Ask for 3 alternative outlines, choose the best and correct it by hand.
5. **Write one chapter at a time.** Give the AI the table of contents, the chapter to write, your notes and experiences. Ask for a draft, then rewrite it in your own voice, adding your own examples.
6. **Check the facts.** Every number, regulation, health or legal tip must be checked against reliable sources.
7. **Add practical tools.** Checklists, tables, exercises, templates: these are what readers perceive as value.
8. **Get it read.** At least two people from your target audience. Ask: "What didn't you understand? What did you skip? What would you use tomorrow?"
9. **Lay it out and create the cover.** A title that's readable even as a thumbnail, a subtitle that states the result.
10. **Prepare the sales page.** Title, who it's for, what it contains, what it doesn't, table of contents, free excerpt, author, price, guarantee.
11. **Upload it to a sales platform** and make a test purchase.

## How to find your first customers

- **Your network.** Announce the project *while you're writing it*: people who follow the process are more likely to buy.
- **Waiting list.** Create a free page (for example with your email marketing tool) where people leave their email to be notified at launch.
- **Valuable free content.** Publish excerpts, checklists and tips from the ebook on social media or in groups where your readers are, following the groups' rules.
- **Collaborations.** Offer professionals with a related audience (not competitors) an exchange: you offer free content to their followers, they mention the ebook.

## How to set prices

The price of an ebook sold directly depends on how specific it is and what result it promises:

| Type of ebook | Common price range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Short generic guide (20–40 pages) | €5–12 | Lots of free competition |
| Specific guide with practical tools | €15–29 | The value is in the specificity |
| Professional guide with templates and resources | €29–59 | For people who earn or save money with it |

These ranges come from observing the market and are indicative. A simple method: look at 10 similar products, discard the most expensive and the cheapest, and position yourself based on how much more specific and complete yours is.

## How to make your first sale

1. Before launch, personally contact 20 people who match your ideal reader (not just friends) and offer the ebook at a launch price in exchange for honest feedback.
2. On launch day, email your waiting list with an offer valid for 72 hours.
3. Publish content that shows **a real page** of the ebook and the problem it solves.
4. Answer every question publicly: questions are your best market research.

## Complete practical example

**Marta** (a fictional character) is an office administrator who, over the years, has learned to manage the family budget with a simple spreadsheet-based method.

1. **Reader:** young couples with small children who can't manage to save.
2. **Promise:** "Your family budget in 30 minutes a month: a method with a ready-made spreadsheet."
3. **Research:** in parenting groups she finds recurring questions about unexpected expenses, utility bills and grocery spending.
4. **Product:** a 45-page ebook + a template spreadsheet + 4 monthly checklists.
5. **AI used for:** the table of contents, chapter drafts from her voice notes, revision, budget examples with hypothetical figures, a cover in Canva.
6. **Price:** €17.
7. **Sales:** Payhip (free plan, 5% fee) + newsletter.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — Marta's numbers.**
Price €17. Platform fee 5% = €0.85. Payment processing (assumption: 3% + €0.30) = about €0.81. Income per copy before taxes: about €15.34.
If she sold 40 copies in the first quarter (an assumption, not a forecast), she would take in about €614 gross. From this she'd need to subtract: subscriptions (for example, 2 months of an AI assistant at about $20 = about €37), taxes and social contributions under her tax regime, and any accountant fees. Time also needs to be considered: about 60 hours of work.
The realistic result of a first product isn't "an income," but an **asset** that can keep selling, plus **market knowledge** for the next products.
:::

### What we learned
- An ebook sells if it takes a specific reader from a problem to a result.
- AI speeds up structure, drafts and design, but the value is your verified experience.
- Platforms that act as merchant of record simplify international VAT.
- Your first ebook is above all an asset and a market test.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Writing about topics you don't know, relying only on AI.
- Generic titles ("A Guide to Saving Money") instead of specific promises.
- Publishing without getting real readers to read it.
- Launching without a waiting list or promotion channel.
- Ignoring the calculation of fees and taxes.

### Practical exercise
Write the sentence "This ebook is for [who] who wants [result] but is stuck because of [problem]" for three different topics. Then use an AI assistant to generate 10 titles and 10 subtitles for the one you find most convincing. Choose the best combination and ask three people from your target audience whether they would buy it, and why.

- [ ] I've chosen a topic in which I have verifiable experience.
- [ ] I've collected at least 15 real questions from the audience.
- [ ] I have an approved table of contents and a specific title.
- [ ] I've calculated net income per copy.
- [ ] I have an active waiting-list page.

# Creating Digital Products and Templates {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

**Digital products** are files the customer downloads or accesses online and uses to get a task done. This category includes: Canva templates, ready-made spreadsheets (budgets, client management, planning), printable planners, document templates (contracts to be checked by a lawyer, quotes, procedures), social media kits, presentations, Notion templates, professional checklists.

The difference from an ebook is that the customer isn't buying **information to read** but **a tool to use**. That's an advantage: the value is immediate and easy to show in a preview.

## Why someone would pay

A good template saves hours of work and reduces errors. A hairdresser who buys 30 Canva post templates isn't paying for the graphics as such: they're paying not to have to design from scratch every week. A freelancer who buys a spreadsheet for quotes and invoices is paying for order and peace of mind.

People pay when the product is:

- **specific to a trade or situation** (templates for physical therapists, not "social media templates");
- **ready to use**, with clear instructions;
- **customizable** without technical skills;
- **well presented**, with previews that show the finished result.

## Tools

| Stage | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Ideas and copy | Free AI assistants | AI assistants, Pro plans |
| Design and templates | Free Canva, Google Slides, Figma (free plan) | Canva Pro (required if you use Pro elements in templates you sell) |
| Spreadsheets | Google Sheets, LibreOffice Calc | Microsoft 365 |
| Digital workspaces | Notion (free plan) | Paid Notion plans |
| Previews and mockups | Canva, free mockups with a commercial license | Paid mockup libraries |
| Sales | Payhip, Gumroad, Etsy | Stan Store, your own website |

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** If you sell Canva templates, read Canva's license terms: in general you can sell templates shared via a link that the customer edits in their own account, but you can't resell Canva design elements as standalone files. Rules change: always check the current terms before selling.
:::

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time for your first product | 1–3 weeks part-time |
| Skills required | Knowledge of the customer's work, a basic sense of design, attention to detail |
| Skills AI supplements | Ideas, sample copy, spreadsheet formulas, user instructions, descriptions |
| Initial investment | €0–30 (an optional month of Canva Pro or a Pro assistant) |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Choose a trade or situation you know.** The better you know the customer's daily work, the better the product will be.
2. **List that customer's repetitive tasks:** what do they redo every week?
3. **Choose one task** and design the tool that simplifies it.
4. **Use AI for the structure.** For example: "Design the structure of a spreadsheet for a dog groomer who needs to manage appointments, clients, services and income. Specify columns, formulas and checks."
5. **Build the product** and **test it yourself** with realistic data.
6. **Write the instructions for use**: a 2–4 page PDF with images, or a short video.
7. **Create 5–8 previews** showing the product in use.
8. **Have it tested** by 2–3 people in the trade and make corrections.
9. **Publish** on a platform and test the purchase.

## How to find your first customers

- Industry groups and communities (following their rules): offer a free reduced version in exchange for feedback.
- Content that shows **before and after**: "This is how I used to organize appointments; this is how I organize them now."
- Pinterest, for visual products (planners, design templates): it works like a visual search engine.
- Collaborations with people who train those professionals (courses, trade associations).

## How to set prices

| Product | Common range | Price lever |
|---|---|---|
| Single template or planner | €3–9 | Speed, design |
| Template bundle (20–50 pieces) | €15–39 | Quantity + consistency |
| Management spreadsheet | €15–49 | Hours saved, fewer errors |
| Professional kit (templates + guide + video) | €39–99 | A complete result for one trade |

Offer **a basic version and a complete version**: many customers choose the intermediate or complete one if the difference in value is clear.

## How to make your first sale

1. Prepare a **free version** (a single template) to give away in exchange for an email address.
2. Three days after someone downloads it, send an email with a usage example and an offer on the complete bundle.
3. Contact 10 professionals in the chosen trade directly, offering the product as a trial in exchange for an honest review.

## Complete practical example

**Luca** (a fictional character) works as a receptionist at a physical therapy practice and knows well the communication problems of small practices.

1. **Product:** "Communication kit for physical therapy practices": 40 Canva templates for posts and stories, 12 message templates for appointment reminders, a 12-page PDF guide on what to publish each month.
2. **AI used for:** a list of patient-education topics, draft copy (then checked by a physical therapist), instructions for use, product descriptions.
3. **Ethical rule:** no content promises recovery; health-related texts were checked by a professional.
4. **Price:** basic version (20 templates) €19, complete €39.
5. **Channels:** Payhip + a professional group + a collaboration with a trainer in the field.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — Luca's numbers.**
Complete version at €39: Payhip fee 5% (€1.95) + payment processing (assumption: 3% + €0.30 = €1.47) → about €35.58 per sale.
If in six months he sold 25 complete versions and 30 basic versions (an assumption), gross income would be about €1,400, from which he'd subtract Canva Pro, any fee paid to the physical therapist for review, taxes and social contributions. Estimated creation time is 50–70 hours, plus customer support.
:::

## Action plan for the first 7 days

| Day | Activity | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose 2 trades you know; list 10 repetitive tasks for each | Task list |
| 2 | Interview (even by message) 3 people in the trade | Confirmed problems |
| 3 | Design the tool with AI | Product structure |
| 4–5 | Build the first version and test it with realistic data | Prototype |
| 6 | Write instructions for use and create previews | Sales materials |
| 7 | Send the prototype to 3 testers | First feedback |

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- Digital products sell tools to use, not information to read.
- Specificity to a trade is the most important factor.
- Clear instructions and well-made previews are part of the product.
- A basic and a complete version help customers choose.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Creating generic templates identical to thousands of others.
- Selling products you haven't tested yourself.
- Violating the licenses of design tools or images.
- Including health, legal or tax copy that hasn't been checked by a professional.

### Practical exercise
Choose a trade you know. List 10 tasks that professional repeats every week. For the three most annoying ones, write which tool (template, spreadsheet, checklist) could simplify them and how much time it would save. Use AI to design the structure of the first one.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've chosen a specific trade.
- [ ] I've spoken with at least 3 people in that trade.
- [ ] I have a working, tested prototype.
- [ ] I've read the license terms of the tools I use.
- [ ] I've prepared instructions for use and previews.
:::

# Selling on Etsy {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

**Etsy** is an international marketplace for handmade products, vintage items, craft supplies and **digital products** (instant downloads). For many beginners it's the first place to sell printables, planners, templates, invitations, graphics and other files, because it already brings an audience of buyers actively looking for products.

It works like this: you open a shop, create listings (title, photos, description, tags, price, file), and Etsy shows your products in its search results. When a customer buys a digital product, they receive the file automatically.

## Etsy's rules on AI (updated November 2026)

This is the first thing to understand, because it has changed recently:

- Etsy's **Creativity Standards** (in force since 2024) only allow you to sell items you have **made, designed, handpicked or sourced** yourself. Digital products you design, even with the help of AI, fall under the "Designed by" category.
- You must **disclose** when a product was created with AI, and you can't present it as handmade.
- With the update of **22 October 2026**, Etsy **banned AI-generated "functional guides"**: patterns (for example knitting or sewing), recipes and instructions. Disclosing the use of AI isn't enough to make them allowed. Anyone selling guides of this kind must be able to show the result with a real item made by following the instructions.
- Listings created by bots without human involvement are prohibited.
- Digital downloads must include a preview or excerpt of the content.

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** News about these rules comes from Etsy's announcement and press coverage (for example CNBC, 22 October 2026). Before opening your shop, read the **Creativity Standards** and the **House Rules** directly on etsy.com: they're the only binding source.
:::

**Practical consequence:** on Etsy it makes sense to sell digital products in which your **creative and design contribution** is clear (design, selection, composition, tested functionality), and not informational content or instructions generated by AI.

## Why someone would pay

People who buy on Etsy are looking for ready-made, beautiful, personalized solutions: a coordinated wedding invitation, a study planner, a pantry label kit, a menu template for a restaurant. They pay for **design**, **consistency**, **practicality** and **time saved**.

## Tools

| Stage | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Niche research | Etsy search bar (suggestions), Pinterest, Google Trends | Etsy SEO tools (eRank, Marmalead, with limited free plans) |
| Design | Canva, Figma, Google Slides | Canva Pro, Affinity, Adobe |
| AI images | Generators with free plans | Midjourney, subscriptions with more credits |
| Mockups | Canva, free mockups with a commercial license | Mockup packs |
| Listing copy | AI assistants | AI assistants, Pro plans |

## Etsy fees (November 2026)

| Item | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Listing fee | $0.20 | Renews every 4 months or with each sale |
| Transaction fee | 6.5% | On the total price (including shipping, if any) |
| Payment processing | Percentage + fixed fee | Varies by country: check the table for your country in Etsy's "Fees" guide |
| Offsite Ads | 15% (12% above $10,000 in sales over 12 months) | Only on sales generated by external ads; mandatory above certain thresholds |
| VAT on fees | Applied to sellers in the EU | Etsy's fees are subject to VAT |
| Shop opening | A one-time fee may apply in some countries | Check when you sign up |

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to open a shop with 10 listings | 2–4 weeks part-time |
| Time to first sales | Often weeks or months: new shops get little visibility |
| Skills required | A sense of design, attention to detail, keyword research |
| Initial investment | From a few euros (listings) to €50–100 (listings + tools + optional ads) |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Study the niche.** Type words like "planner," "template," "printable" into Etsy's search and note the automatic suggestions: they're real user searches.
2. **Analyze 20 products that sell** in the chosen niche: price, number of reviews, photo style, what customers complain about in negative reviews.
3. **Find a different angle.** A more specific audience (primary school teachers, wedding planners, people with a balcony vegetable garden), a different style, an extra feature.
4. **Create a consistent collection** of 10–15 products in the same style, rather than random products.
5. **Create the shop**: name, logo, banner, information, policies (for digital downloads, refunds are generally not offered except for defective files: state this clearly).
6. **Prepare the listings**:
   - a title with the main keywords, written for people and not just for the algorithm;
   - 13 tags (the maximum allowed) with different keywords;
   - 5–10 images: a first image that's clear even as a thumbnail, then details, use, format, instructions;
   - a description: what the buyer receives, formats, sizes, how to use it, what's *not* included, a disclosure of AI use where relevant.
7. **Upload the files** and make a test purchase (or check the download with the preview).

## How to find your first customers

- **Etsy's internal search:** this is the main channel. Precise titles and tags matter more than anything.
- **Pinterest:** create vertical pins for each product, linked to the listing.
- **Personal network and social media:** the first reviews are the hardest; ask only people who have actually bought and used the product, without offering compensation (incentivized reviews break the rules).
- **Etsy Ads:** consider them only after optimizing your listings, starting with very small budgets and measuring results.

## How to set prices

Always calculate the **net per sale**.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — calculating net income on Etsy.**
Digital product at €8.00. Listing: about €0.19 ($0.20). Transaction 6.5%: €0.52. Payment processing (assumption for the calculation: 4% + €0.30): €0.62. 22% VAT on fees (assumption for a seller in Italy): about €0.29.
Net per sale before taxes: about **€6.38**, roughly 20% less than the price. On €3 products the impact of the fixed fees is much higher: that's why very cheap products earn little.
:::

## How to make your first sale

1. Publish at least 10–15 consistent listings: a shop with a single product converts poorly.
2. Optimize the listings after 2 weeks by looking at the statistics (visits, favorites): change the first image or the title of those that get no visits.
3. Promote 3 products on Pinterest and in your network.
4. Offer a **personalization** service (for example an invitation with the couple's names): first sales often come from customers who want something made to measure.

## Complete practical example

**Giulia** (a fictional character) is a primary school teacher with a passion for graphic design.

1. **Niche:** printable materials for classroom organization in Italian primary schools (reading logs, class calendars, labels, end-of-year certificates).
2. **Differentiation:** products in Italian, aligned with the Italian school calendar, a recognizable graphic style, editable files.
3. **AI used for:** product ideas, decorative elements generated and then retouched, listing copy, 13 tags per listing.
4. **Human contribution:** layouts designed and tested in class, choice of the educational content, revision.
5. **Disclosure:** her listings state that the decorative illustrations were created with the help of AI.
6. **Initial collection:** 12 products between €3.50 and €9; an annual bundle at €24.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — the first three months.**
Costs: 12 listings (about €2.30), one month of Canva Pro, one month of an AI assistant. If Giulia sold 35 products at an average price of €6 and 5 annual bundles (an assumption), gross income would be €330, and the net after fees about €255–265, before taxes and social contributions. The most important work, however, is understanding which products get visits and favorites, to guide the next collection.
:::

### What we learned
- Etsy brings buyers who are actively searching, but new shops get little initial visibility.
- The rules on AI are strict: disclosure is mandatory and AI-generated functional guides are banned.
- Net per sale must be calculated including fixed fees and VAT on fees.
- Consistent, specific collections work better than random products.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Selling recipes, patterns or instructions generated with AI.
- Not disclosing AI use or presenting items as "handmade."
- Copying other sellers' products or using protected brands and characters.
- Prices so low that fixed fees make them uneconomical.
- Asking for reviews in exchange for compensation.

### Practical exercise
Choose a niche. Analyze 20 competing listings by filling in a table with: title, price, number of reviews, style of the first image, complaints in negative reviews. Identify three "gaps" in the offering and write a product idea for each.
