# Building Websites and Landing Pages {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

A **website** presents a business online; a **landing page** is a single page designed for one goal only: collecting contacts, selling a product, getting people to book a service.

Today there are website builders that let you create professional sites without coding, and many include AI features: they generate a first version of the site from a description, write copy, suggest layouts and images. AI assistants can also write HTML code for simple pages. The service you offer the client is to **design, build, publish and maintain** the site, making sure it works, is clear, fast, mobile-friendly and compliant with the rules.

## Why someone would pay

Even with AI tools, most small business owners don't want to deal with domains, structure, copy, images, contact forms, privacy policies, cookies, connecting to Google, updates. They pay to get **a site that brings in contacts** without losing weeks over it.

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to get started | 3–6 weeks to learn a tool and create 2 demo sites |
| Skills | Content structure, basic design, attention to mobile use, notions of local SEO, privacy and cookies |
| Investment | €0–30/month for your own site and tools; the costs of clients' sites are paid by them |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Choose a single builder** and learn it well.
2. **Learn the structure of an effective page**:
   - at the top: who you are, who it's for, what result, action button;
   - benefits and services;
   - proof (real reviews, real photos, certifications);
   - how it works (3 steps);
   - frequently asked questions;
   - contact details and map;
   - legal information (privacy, cookies, VAT number where required).
3. **Use AI for**: the site map, draft copy, FAQ sections, alt text for images (accessibility), meta descriptions for Google.
4. **Build 2 demo sites** for fictional businesses, labeled as examples.
5. **Prepare a pre-publication checklist**: speed, mobile version, working forms, links, privacy and cookies, connection to Google Search Console and Google Business Profile.
6. **Define the maintenance service** (updates, small changes, monthly checks).

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** In Italy and the EU, a website that collects data (contact forms, newsletters, analytics, profiling cookies) must comply with the GDPR and cookie rules: a privacy notice, a compliant cookie banner when needed, consent for marketing communications. Use reliable policy generators or have the texts checked by a professional. Don't copy another site's privacy notice.
:::

## How to find your first clients

- Local businesses without a website or with a site that doesn't work on phones (check with your own phone).
- Professionals launching a new service (landing page).
- Digital product creators (sales pages).
- Collaborations with social media managers, photographers and accountants.

## How to set prices

| Brochure site, 4–6 pages | €600–2,000 |
| Simple online store | €1,200–3,500 |
| Maintenance | €30–100/month |
| Optimizing the Google Business Profile | €100–250 one-off |

Indicative market ranges. Always distinguish **your fee** from **third-party costs** (domain, builder plan, plugins, images), which the client pays directly or which you re-invoice transparently.

## How to make your first sale

Offer a clear package with no surprises: "Landing page for your new service: copy, design, contact form, connection to Google, published within 10 days, 2 revisions, €450." Show the demo site on your phone, because that's where their customers will see it.

## Complete practical example

**Federico** (a fictional character) is an office worker with a passion for technology.

1. **Niche:** tradespeople (plumbers, electricians, carpenters) in his province.
2. **Offer:** "Website in 7 days": one page with services, area covered, real photos of jobs, real Google reviews, call and WhatsApp buttons, a quote request form; optimization of the Google Business Profile.
3. **Tools:** an AI-powered builder for the first structure, an AI assistant for copy, Canva for graphics.
4. **Price:** €490 + optional maintenance at €30/month; domain and builder plan paid by the client.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — Federico's numbers.**
Time per site: about 12–15 hours. If he built 2 sites a month (an assumption) and 6 clients took up maintenance, monthly revenue would be about €1,160. From this, subtract his own tools, taxes and social contributions. The time spent finding clients (visits, calls, quotes) can equal or exceed the time spent building: it must be accounted for.
:::

### What we learned
- AI-powered builders make it possible to create professional sites without coding.
- Clients pay for a site that brings in contacts, not for the technology.
- Privacy, cookies and mobile performance are part of the service.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Beautiful sites without a clear action for the visitor.
- Forgetting the mobile version.
- Ignoring privacy and cookies.
- Mixing your fee with third-party costs without transparency.
- Registering the domain in your name instead of the client's.

### Practical exercise

# Creating Online Courses and Educational Materials {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

An **online course** is a structured learning path: video lessons, texts, exercises, downloadable materials, quizzes, sometimes live sessions or a community. **Educational materials** also include worksheets, workbooks, presentations and programs for schools, companies or trainers.

AI helps design the curriculum, write lesson scripts, create quizzes and exercises, and generate slides, captions and translations. But a course sells if the teacher is **credible** and if the path really leads to a result.

## Why someone would pay

People pay for a course when:

- the result is **concrete** ("Learn to use Excel for your café's bookkeeping in 6 weeks");
- the path is **orderly** and saves time compared with free content;
- there's **support** (questions, feedback, community);
- the teacher has **demonstrable experience**.

## Tools

| Stage | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Design | Free AI assistants | AI assistants, Pro plans |
| Slides | Google Slides, Canva | Canva Pro, Gamma (paid plans: check) |
| Recording | OBS Studio (free), built-in screen recorder | Descript, Loom |
| Video and captions | CapCut, DaVinci Resolve | CapCut Pro, Descript |
| Course platform | Unlisted YouTube + Payhip, Google Classroom (for education) | Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi, Podia (monthly plans) |
| Quizzes | Google Forms | Built-in platform features |

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time for your first course | 4–10 weeks part-time for a short, quality course |
| Skills | Command of the subject, clear explanations, instructional design |
| Investment | €0–150 (microphone, platform, subscriptions) |

## Instructional design in 5 rules

1. **Start from the end result**: what will the student be able to *do* at the end?
2. **Divide into short modules**: 5–12 minute lessons with a single objective each.
3. **Every lesson has an action**: an exercise, an assignment, a checklist.
4. **Check learning**: quizzes, projects, feedback.
5. **Cut the unnecessary**: anything that doesn't serve the result goes.

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Validate the idea before creating the course**: offer a **live online workshop** (free or low-cost) on the topic. If people attend and ask questions, the topic is of interest.
2. **Write the curriculum** with AI, starting from the end result and the questions collected in the workshop.
3. **Pre-sell** the course at a launch price to a small group (a "pilot edition") with lessons released weekly and live sessions.
4. **Record the lessons** as you go, improving them with students' questions.
5. **Create materials**: slides, worksheets, exercises, quizzes.
6. **Collect feedback** and **real** testimonials (with consent).
7. **Publish the recorded version** on a platform.

## How to find your first students

- Your existing audience (colleagues, social media, newsletter).
- Free workshops as an entry point.
- Companies and trade associations (staff training).
- Schools, libraries, training centers (for educational materials and in-person courses).

## How to set prices

| Format | Indicative range |
|---|---|
| Recorded mini-course (1–2 hours) | €19–79 |
| Complete recorded course with materials | €97–297 |
| Course with live sessions and feedback | €197–797 |
| Corporate training (half day) | €300–1,200 depending on sector and number of participants |

Indicative market ranges. Personal support increases perceived value more than extra content.

## How to make your first sale

The pilot edition is the safest route: sell to 10–20 people before recording everything, at a reduced price, with the promise of personal guidance. That way you earn, learn and create a better course.

## Complete practical example

**Roberta** (a fictional character) is a bookkeeper who helps small shop owners keep their accounts in order.

1. **Course:** "Excel for small shops: income, expenses and margins in 5 weeks."
2. **Validation:** a free 60-minute online workshop, 35 sign-ups, 22 attendees, lots of questions about inventory management.
3. **Pilot edition:** 12 participants at €97, 5 live sessions + exercises.
4. **AI use:** curriculum, quizzes, exercise files with hypothetical data, draft slides, captions for the recordings.
5. **Recorded version:** €149, with template files and a monthly Q&A session.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — Roberta's pilot edition.**
12 × €97 = €1,164 gross. Costs: video conferencing platform, payment system (about 3% + a fixed fee), AI tools. Time: about 40 hours across preparation, sessions and feedback. The main value is a course validated by real students and material for future editions.
:::

## Action plan for the first 7 days

| Day | Activity | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose the course result and audience | Promise |
| 2 | Collect 20 real questions on the topic | Needs map |
| 3 | Design a 60-minute workshop with AI | Outline |
| 4 | Create the workshop slides | Material |
| 5 | Publish the invitation and open registration | Registration page |
| 6 | Personally invite 30 people | Sign-ups |
| 7 | Prepare the pilot edition curriculum | Pilot offer |

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- A course sells a concrete result, an orderly path and support.
- Validating with a workshop and a pilot edition reduces risk.
- AI speeds up the curriculum, materials and quizzes; credibility and clarity remain yours.
- Personal support increases the course's value.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Recording an entire course before knowing whether anyone wants it.
- Lessons that are too long and lack practical actions.
- Promising students financial results.
- Using invented or unauthorized testimonials.

### Practical exercise
Write your course's result using the formula "By the end of this course you'll be able to [concrete action] even if [obstacle]." Use AI to create a curriculum of 5 modules with 3 lessons each, then remove everything that doesn't directly serve the result.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I have a concrete, measurable course result.
- [ ] I've collected real questions from the audience.
- [ ] I've designed a validation workshop.
- [ ] Every planned lesson has a practical action.
- [ ] I have a plan for the pilot edition.
:::

# Starting a Small AI Services Agency {.chapter}

## What it is and how it works

An **AI services agency** helps small and medium-sized businesses use artificial intelligence in concrete ways. It's not about developing complex technology, but about offering services such as:

- **training** staff to use AI assistants;
- **setting up** tools (custom assistants with the company's documents, prompt templates, workflows);
- **automating** repetitive tasks (for example: requests from the website form land in a spreadsheet, AI drafts a reply, the manager approves it);
- **producing content** (social media, copy, images, video) on the client's behalf;
- **chatbots** to answer frequently asked questions on the website;
- **consulting** on internal AI-use policies, privacy and transparency.

At first the "agency" can be one person combining two or three of these services and working with other freelancers for the rest.

## Why someone would pay

Small businesses hear about AI every day but:

- they don't know **where to start**;
- they fear **mistakes and risks** (privacy, confidential data, embarrassing moments with customers);
- they don't have **time** to experiment;
- they want **measurable results**: hours saved, faster replies, more leads.

## Tools

| Category | Tools | Notes (November 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Assistants for businesses | ChatGPT Business/Enterprise, Claude Team/Enterprise, Gemini for Google Workspace, Microsoft Copilot | Business plans with contractual data safeguards: check prices and terms on the official websites |
| Automation | Zapier (free: 100 tasks/month), Make (free: 1,000 credits/month), n8n (open source, self-hostable) | Paid plans based on volume |
| Website chatbots | Built-in features of website builders, chatbot platforms | Consider privacy and control over answers |
| Documentation | Notion, Google Docs | Manuals and procedures for the client |
| Content | The tools from the previous chapters | — |

## Time, skills and investment

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Time to get started | 1–3 months to build skills, an offer and first cases |
| Skills | Advanced use of AI assistants, basic automation, ability to analyze business processes, communication, notions of privacy |
| Investment | €50–200/month on tools, training and testing |

## Step-by-step procedure to get started

1. **Become competent in one area** before selling: for example "custom AI assistants for administrative offices" or "automating quote requests."
2. **Choose a sector**: professional firms, restaurants, real estate agencies, shops, accommodation businesses. Knowing a sector's processes is a great advantage.
3. **Map the sector's typical processes**: which tasks are repetitive, text-based, with clear rules? Those are the candidates for AI.
4. **Design 3 fixed-price services** (see the pricing table).
5. **Create demo cases** on your own work or on a company you know (with permission): measure time before and after.
6. **Prepare professional documents**: proposal, contract, data processing agreement (if you process personal data on the client's behalf you are a **data processor** under the GDPR), usage guidelines for the client's employees.
7. **Define support** after delivery.

::: {.box .warn}
**Warning.** When you set up AI tools for a business, check where the data goes, whether it's used for training, who can access it and which plans offer contractual safeguards. Never upload personal data of your client's customers into tools on consumer plans. In the EU, the AI Act also requires companies that use AI systems to ensure a sufficient level of **AI literacy** among their staff: training is a useful service for this too.
:::

## How to find your first clients

- **Personal and professional network**: your employer, former colleagues, suppliers, trade associations.
- **Events and workshops**: offer a free session, "AI in practice for [sector]," at associations, chambers of commerce, coworking spaces.
- **LinkedIn**: publish practical cases with real numbers from your own work (time saved).
- **Partners**: accountants, IT consultants and web agencies that don't offer AI services.

## How to set prices

| Service | Indicative range |
|---|---|
| Training workshop (3–4 hours, small group) | €400–1,200 |
| Setting up a custom assistant + user guide | €500–1,500 |
| Automating a process (analysis, build, testing, documentation) | €600–2,500 |
| Monthly support and continuous improvement | €150–600/month |
| Content production on a subscription | See the relevant chapters |

Indicative market ranges. Also base the price on **value** to the client: if an automation saves an employee 5 hours a week, the annual saving is easy to estimate together with the client.

## How to make your first sale

1. Offer a **free 45-minute analysis**: interview the owner about their processes, identify 3 opportunities, estimate the time saved.
2. Send a written proposal with a small, fixed-price **pilot project** with a measurable result.
3. After the pilot, present the results and propose monthly support or the next project.

## Complete practical example

**Simone** (a fictional character) has worked for eight years in the administration of a logistics company.

1. **Sector:** small accounting and payroll consultancy firms (3–10 people).
2. **Services:**
   - the "Safe AI in the office" workshop (3 hours): using assistants, what never to enter, sample prompts for emails, client circulars, summaries of regulations (always to be checked);
   - setting up an assistant with the firm's internal procedures, on a business plan chosen together with the firm;
   - automation: client requests from the website form are classified and receive a draft reply for approval.
3. **Price:** workshop €600, setup €900, automation €1,200, support €250/month.

::: {.box .ipotesi}
**Hypothetical example — Simone's first six months.**
If in six months he sold 4 workshops, 2 setups and 1 automation, with 2 clients on support for 3 months (an assumption), revenue would be about €6,300. Costs: tools and testing (about €100/month), recommended professional liability insurance, an accountant, taxes and social contributions. Client acquisition in a cautious sector like professional firms can take a long time: many negotiations need several meetings.
:::

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- An AI services agency helps SMEs with training, setup, automation and content.
- Specializing by sector and having measurable cases are the main levers.
- Privacy, business plans and AI literacy are integral to the service.
- Fixed-price pilot projects reduce clients' hesitation.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Selling services you can't yet deliver well.
- Promising savings or results you haven't measured.
- Using consumer plans with clients' confidential data.
- Building automations without documentation and support.
- Ignoring your role as data processor.

### Practical exercise
Choose a repetitive task from your current job (for example answering similar email requests). Describe the process step by step, identify where AI can help, build a simple solution (even just a saved prompt with reply templates) and measure the time before and after for a week.

- [ ] I have a demo case with measured time.
- [ ] I have three fixed-price services.
- [ ] I know the basic obligations on data and privacy.
- [ ] I have templates for the free analysis and the proposal.
