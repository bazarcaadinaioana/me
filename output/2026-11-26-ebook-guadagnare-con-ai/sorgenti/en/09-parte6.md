# Prompt Library {.part}

This library contains 55 professional prompts, organized into ten categories. Each one is ready to copy: replace the parts in square brackets with your own information. The more detail you include, the better the result.

# 55 Ready-to-Use Prompts {.chapter}

## How to use these prompts

1. **Copy** the prompt into an AI assistant.
2. **Replace** every part in [square brackets].
3. **Add** documents or examples when the prompt suggests it.
4. **Iterate**: ask for targeted changes to the result.
5. **Always check** facts, numbers and names before using the result.

::: {.box .tip}
**Tip.** Many prompts end with "If you're missing information, ask me questions first." Keep this sentence for important work: it greatly improves quality.
:::

## Business

### 1. Exploring ideas from your skills

**When to use it:** at the start, to find business ideas consistent with your profile.

```text
Here's my profile: work experience [..], skills [..],
serious hobbies [..], problems I've solved for myself or others [..],
time available per week [..], starting budget [..].
Suggest 20 ideas for services or digital products I can offer using
AI as a tool. For each: customer, problem, format, why my profile
is an advantage, a concrete first step. Exclude ideas anyone could
replicate in a day without skills.
```

### 2. Evaluating an idea critically

**When to use it:** before investing time in an idea.

```text
Act as a skeptical but constructive business consultant.
Idea: [description]. Customer: [who]. Planned price: [..].
Analyze: 1) the 5 main reasons it might not work;
2) which assumptions I should test first and how (low-cost tests);
3) the signals that would tell me to continue or stop.
Don't be accommodating.
```

### 3. A one-page business model

**When to use it:** to bring order to a project.

```text
Create a one-page business model for [business].
Sections: customers, problem, value proposition, channels, customer
relationships, revenue, key activities, resources, partners, costs.
For each section write 2-4 concrete points. At the end, list the
3 riskiest assumptions. If you're missing information, ask me questions.
```

### 4. Calculating your minimum rate

**When to use it:** to set the price of a service.

```text
Help me calculate my minimum hourly rate. Monthly net goal:
€[..]. Estimated billable hours per month: [..]. Monthly fixed costs: [..].
Estimated percentage for taxes and social contributions (to be checked
with my accountant): [..]%. Show the calculation step by step in a
table, then show what happens if billable hours drop by 25%.
```

### 5. A 90-day action plan

**When to use it:** after choosing your business.

```text
Create a 90-day plan to launch [business], dedicating [..] hours
a week. Divide it into 3 phases of 30 days, each with a measurable
goal. For each week: 3 priority tasks and the expected result.
Include checkpoints and decision moments.
```

### 6. Preparing for a negotiation

**When to use it:** before meeting a client or supplier.

```text
I need to negotiate [subject] with [type of counterpart]. My goal:
[..]. My limit: [..]. What I know about the other party: [..].
Prepare: 1) the 5 most likely objections with a response for each;
2) 3 concessions I can offer without lowering the price;
3) a role-play: you play the difficult counterpart, I respond.
Start the role-play after the analysis.
```

## Marketing

### 7. Ideal customer profile

**When to use it:** before writing any promotional copy.

```text
Create the ideal customer profile for [product/service], based on
this real information gathered from interviews and reviews: [..].
Include: situation, goals, frustrations, objections to buying,
where they look for information, words they use (quote the real
sentences provided). Clearly flag what is inferred and what comes
from the information.
```

### 8. Value proposition

**When to use it:** for the main line of your website, profile or presentation.

```text
Write 10 versions of the value proposition for [product/service]
using the formula "[Product] helps [customer] to [result] without
[obstacle]." Vary the style: direct, emotional, numerical, question.
Then choose the best 3 and explain why.
```

### 9. A one-month marketing strategy

**When to use it:** to plan your promotion.

```text
Create a 4-week marketing strategy for [offer], audience [..],
ad budget €[..], available channels [..].
Organize it by customer journey stage (discovery, interest, trust,
purchase). For each week: goal, actions, content, indicators to
measure. Include a free resource to collect emails.
```

### 10. Ideas for a free resource

**When to use it:** to build your email list.

```text
Suggest 10 ideas for free resources (checklists, templates,
mini-guides, calculators) to attract [audience] interested in [topic],
consistent with the paid product [..]. For each: title, contents
in 5 points, why it leads naturally to the paid product.
```

### 11. Welcome email sequence

**When to use it:** after someone joins your list.

```text
Write a sequence of 5 welcome emails for people who download
[resource]. Email 1: delivery and first action. Email 2: my story
and why I do this work (use this information: [..]). Email 3: a
practical tip. Email 4: a common mistake. Email 5: introducing
[offer]. Subject line and preview text for each. Tone [..].
Maximum 200 words per email.
```

### 12. Analyzing marketing results

**When to use it:** at the end of the month.

```text
Here is this month's data [paste or attach]. Analyze: which
content/channels brought the most [goal], what they have in common,
what performed worst. Suggest 5 concrete actions for next month.
Distinguish facts in the data from your interpretations.
```

## Social media

### 13. Monthly editorial plan

**When to use it:** every month.

```text
Create the editorial plan for [month] for [account], audience [..],
goal [..]. [N] pieces: [formats]. Pillars: [..].
In a table: date, format, pillar, hook (max 8 words), summary,
visual material needed, call to action. Consider the month's
occasions relevant to the sector.
```

### 14. Hooks for short videos

**When to use it:** to improve the first seconds of your videos.

```text
Write 15 hooks for a short video about [topic] for [audience].
5 as questions, 5 based on a common mistake, 5 with a surprising
but true statement. Maximum 10 words each.
Avoid misleading clickbait.
```

### 15. Reel or TikTok script

**When to use it:** to produce a short video.

```text
Write the script for a [duration]-second video about [topic].
Table format: seconds, what's on screen (filming directions),
what's said, on-screen text (max 6 words). Structure: hook,
problem, solution shown, call to action. Then write the caption
(max 120 words) and 5 specific hashtags.
```

### 16. Educational carousel

**When to use it:** for content people will save.

```text
Create an 8-slide carousel about [topic] for [audience].
Slide 1: a title promising a concrete benefit. Slides 2-7: one point
per slide, short title + 1-2 sentences. Slide 8: summary and an
invitation to save. Also give a visual suggestion for each slide.
```

### 17. Repurposing long-form content

**When to use it:** to turn an article, video or podcast into several pieces.

```text
Here is a long piece of content [text or transcript]. Derive from it:
3 short video ideas with hooks, 2 carousels (slide titles),
5 short posts, 1 newsletter email. Keep only information present
in the original text.
```

### 18. Replies to comments

**When to use it:** to handle difficult or frequent comments.

```text
I manage the profile of [business], tone [..]. Write 3 possible
replies to this comment: "[comment]". One short, one more detailed,
one that moves the conversation to private messages. Never
argumentative, never defensive.
```

## Sales

### 19. A complete sales page

**When to use it:** for a product or service.

```text
Write a draft sales page for [offer], audience [..], price [..].
Sections: headline with the result, subheadline, problem, solution,
what's included (list), who it's for and who it isn't for, about me
(use: [..]), frequently asked questions (8), guarantee/terms, final
call to action. Don't invent testimonials or numbers. Leave
placeholders [REAL TESTIMONIAL] where they would go.
```

### 20. Handling objections

**When to use it:** before presenting an offer.

```text
List the 10 most likely objections [customer] would have to buying
[offer] at [price]. For each, write an honest, short response,
and state which proof (example, guarantee, demonstration) would
make it more convincing.
```

### 21. First-contact message

**When to use it:** to contact potential clients in a personalized way.

```text
Write a first-contact message for [type of client] via [channel].
Information about the recipient: [..]. Structure: personal reference,
useful observation, small and concrete proposal, simple question.
Maximum 90 words. No pushy sales phrases. Write 3 variations.
```

### 22. Business proposal

**When to use it:** after a call with a potential client.

```text
Write a business proposal for [client] based on these call notes:
[..]. Sections: current situation, goals, proposed solution,
activities included and excluded, timeline, investment
(3 options: [..]), next steps. Professional, clear tone.
Maximum 2 pages.
```

### 23. Following up on a quote

**When to use it:** when a client doesn't reply.

```text
Write 3 follow-up messages for a quote sent [..] days ago to
[client] for [service]. First: a polite reminder with added value.
Second (a week later): a question about any doubts. Third: a kind
close that leaves the door open. Maximum 60 words each.
```

### 24. Sales role-play

**When to use it:** to practice before a call.

```text
Play a [type of client] who is interested but skeptical about
[offer]. You have these concerns: [..]. Answer realistically,
one line at a time. At the end of the role-play, when I write
"END," assess my performance with 3 strengths and 3 improvements.
```

## Ebook creation

### 25. An ebook outline

**When to use it:** after defining the reader and the promise.

```text
Suggest 3 alternative outlines for an ebook titled [..] for
[reader], who wants [result] but is stuck because of [problem].
Planned length [..] pages. For each chapter: title, objective,
3-5 subsections, a practical exercise. Indicate where my direct
experience will be needed.
```

### 26. From notes to chapter

**When to use it:** to turn your notes into a draft.

```text
Turn these notes of mine [paste] into the chapter "[title]" of about
[..] words. Keep only the information in the notes; don't add facts,
numbers or examples that weren't provided. Structure: introduction,
sections with subheadings, a tip box, summary. At the end, list the
missing information as questions for me.
```

### 27. Editorial review

**When to use it:** after writing a chapter.

```text
Act as a professional editor. Review this chapter [text].
1) List problems with clarity, repetition and skipped steps.
2) Flag every statement that needs checking or a source.
3) Propose the corrected version, keeping my voice.
List the changes made.
```

### 28. Exercises and checklists

**When to use it:** to make a chapter practical.

```text
For this chapter [text or summary], create: 1 practical exercise with
step-by-step instructions and an estimated time; 1 checklist of 5-7
verifiable points; 3 self-assessment questions. Level: beginners.
```

### 29. Titles and subtitles

**When to use it:** before creating the cover.

```text
Generate 15 titles and 15 subtitles for an ebook about [topic] for
[reader]. Titles: short, memorable. Subtitles: a concrete, specific
promise, with no unrealistic guarantees. Then suggest the 5 best
combinations and explain your reasoning.
```

### 30. Store description

**When to use it:** for the ebook's sales page.

```text
Write the sales description of the ebook [title] for [platform].
Structure: the reader's problem, what they'll learn (list), who it's
for, what it contains (pages, exercises, resources), who the author
is [..]. Maximum [..] words. No superlatives or guaranteed promises.
```

## Copywriting

### 31. Copy with a PAS structure

**When to use it:** for short persuasive copy.

```text
Write a [..]-word text with a PAS structure (Problem, Agitation,
Solution) for [offer], audience [..]. Use these real customer
words: [..]. End with a clear call to action: [..].
```

### 32. Rewriting in a different tone

**When to use it:** to adapt copy to a new audience or channel.

```text
Rewrite this text [..] for [new audience/channel] in a [..] tone.
Keep all the information. Maximum length [..]. Then explain in
3 points what you changed and why.
```

### 33. E-commerce product pages

**When to use it:** for several similar products.

```text
Write the product page for [product] using only this technical
data: [..]. Structure: title (max 70 characters), 2 opening
sentences on the main benefit, 5 bullet points, a paragraph on use
and care, technical data in a table. Tone [..]. Don't add features
that aren't in the data.
```

### 34. Copy for a brochure website

**When to use it:** to create or refresh a website.

```text
Write the copy for [business]'s website: Home, About us, Services
(one section for [services]), FAQ (8 questions), Contact. Real
information: [..]. For each page: headline, subheadline, copy, call
to action. Insert [PLACEHOLDER] for real photos and reviews.
```

### 35. Ads

**When to use it:** for paid campaigns.

```text
Write 5 ad variations for [platform] to promote [offer]
to [audience]. For each: primary text (max 125 characters),
headline (max 40 characters), description (max 30 characters),
visual idea. Each variation uses a different angle: benefit, problem,
proof, curiosity, offer. Follow the platform's advertising rules.
```

### 36. A strict editor's review

**When to use it:** before delivering copy to a client.

```text
Reread this text [..] as a strict editor. Flag: clichés, vague
statements, repetition, unverifiable points, overlong passages.
Score it from 1 to 10 on clarity and persuasiveness. Then rewrite
the improved version.
```

## Images

### 37. A brand style recipe

**When to use it:** to get consistent images.

```text
Create a "style recipe" for [brand]'s images, audience [..],
values [..]. Include: style (photographic/illustrated), a palette of
4-5 colors, lighting, preferred framing, recurring elements, what to
avoid. Then write a reusable base prompt in which I only need to
change the subject.
```

### 38. Lifestyle photos for social media

**When to use it:** for backgrounds and concept scenes (not for showing altered real products).

```text
Realistic lifestyle photograph, [subject/scene], set in [place],
natural [morning/afternoon] light, palette [..], shallow depth of
field, [..] framing, empty space [on the right/at the top] for text,
[..] mood. Format [4:5 / 9:16]. No text, no logos.
```

### 39. Consistent illustrations for a product

**When to use it:** for planners, ebooks, educational materials.

```text
Illustration in [watercolor/flat/continuous line] style, subject
[..], palette [..], white background, [..] outlines, no text.
Same style for a series of [N] illustrations: [list of subjects].
```

### 40. Ebook cover (concept)

**When to use it:** to explore ideas before laying out.

```text
Cover concept for an ebook about [topic] for [audience]. Style
[minimalist/editorial/illustrated], central visual element [..],
palette [..], ample empty space at the top for the title, no text
in the image. Vertical 2:3 format.
```

### 41. A brief for an image for a client

**When to use it:** to gather information before generating.

```text
Prepare a brief for creating the images for the [..] campaign for
[client]. List the questions to ask the client about: goal, format,
use (social, print, website), real products to show, people, brand
constraints, deadlines, usage rights. Then create a fillable brief
template.
```

## Video

### 42. Storyboard for a promotional video

**When to use it:** before filming.

```text
Create the storyboard for a [duration]-second promotional video for
[offer], platform [..]. Table: scene, duration, framing, action,
audio/voice, on-screen text, material needed (real footage or
graphics). The real product must appear within the first [..] seconds.
```

### 43. Voiceover

**When to use it:** for promotional videos or tutorials.

```text
Write the text of a [duration]-second voiceover
(about [..] words) for [video]. Rhythm: short sentences, a pause after
each idea. Tone [..]. Mark pauses and words to emphasize in brackets.
```

### 44. Prompt for a generated clip

**When to use it:** with video generators.

```text
5-second video clip: [subject] in [setting], camera movement
[slow push-in / pan / static], light [..], realistic cinematic style,
colors [..], no text, no recognizable people. Vertical 9:16 format.
```

### 45. Ideas for a video series

**When to use it:** to plan recurring content.

```text
Suggest 3 recurring video series formats for [account], audience
[..]. For each format: name, fixed structure, length, frequency,
10 episodes with titles, why the audience would follow it.
```

### 46. Script for a tutorial video

**When to use it:** for courses or educational content.

```text
Write the script for a [duration]-minute tutorial on [procedure].
Structure: the end result shown at the start, materials needed,
numbered steps (what I say, what I show), common mistakes,
summary. Language for complete beginners.
```

## Market research

### 47. Competitor analysis

**When to use it:** before creating a product.

```text
Here is public information about [N] competitors [paste].
Create a comparison table: offer, price, audience, strengths,
weaknesses, channels. Then identify 3 uncovered market gaps.
Base it only on the information provided.
```

### 48. Review analysis

**When to use it:** to discover unmet needs.

```text
Analyze these [N] reviews [paste]. Group praise and complaints by
theme, with frequency. Quote 2 reviews verbatim per theme.
Conclude with 5 features a new product should have.
```

### 49. Customer interview guide

**When to use it:** before talking to potential customers.

```text
Prepare a guide for interviewing [type of customer] about the problem
[..]. 12 open questions about the past and real behavior
(not hypothetical), in logical order. Add tips for not influencing
the answers and a template for taking notes.
```

### 50. Interview summary

**When to use it:** after the interviews (with anonymized notes).

```text
Here are the anonymized notes from [N] interviews [paste]. Summarize:
problems mentioned with frequency, solutions already tried,
significant verbatim sentences, the main need in one sentence,
assumptions to test.
```

### 51. Checking a niche

**When to use it:** to decide whether a niche is suitable.

```text
Evaluate the niche [description] on: frequency of the problem,
willingness to pay, reachability, competition, size.
For each criterion, state what evidence I should look for and where
(websites, groups, marketplaces, searches), without inventing market data.
```

## Digital products

### 52. Designing a template

**When to use it:** to create templates or planners.

```text
Design a [template/planner] for [user] who needs to [task].
List the pages or sections, what each contains, fields to fill in,
instructions for use, recommended format (A4, digital, Canva, Notion).
Suggest a basic version and a complete version.
```

### 53. A spreadsheet with formulas

**When to use it:** for management products.

```text
Design a spreadsheet for [user] who needs to manage [..].
Specify: sheets, columns, exact Google Sheets formulas, data
validation, conditional formatting, an example with 5 rows of
hypothetical data, and instructions for the end user.
```

### 54. Instructions for the customer

**When to use it:** for every digital product.

```text
Write instructions for use for [product] aimed at people who aren't
comfortable with technology. Include: what you receive, the first
5 minutes, numbered steps, frequently asked questions (6), how to
ask for support. Very simple language. Indicate where to insert
screenshots.
```

### 55. Packaging and pricing

**When to use it:** before publishing.

```text
I've created [product]. Competitors' prices: [..]. Platform fees:
[..]. Suggest 3 offer tiers (basic, standard, complete) with
contents and price, calculate the net per sale of each after fees,
and suggest which to highlight and why.
```

::: {.recap}
### What we learned
- An effective prompt has a role, context, objective, format and limits.
- The parts in square brackets must always be personalized.
- Asking for verbatim quotes and placeholders reduces the risk of invented content.

### Mistakes to avoid
- Using the prompts without personalizing them.
- Publishing results without checking them.
- Entering third parties' personal data into prompts.

### Practical exercise
Choose 5 prompts from this library that are useful for your business, personalize them with your information and save them in your "My prompts" document with a note on the result you got.

### Review checklist
- [ ] I've saved at least 10 personalized prompts.
- [ ] I've tested at least one prompt in each category relevant to me.
- [ ] I've noted which changes improve the results.
:::
